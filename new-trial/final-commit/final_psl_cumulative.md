Released on 23 March 2026 | CEO Kevin Kawchak | ChemicalQDevice

## Regulatory Framework

- ICH E6(R3) Adaption (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18973368) - Good Clinical Practice
  guidelines adapted for Physical AI autonomous clinical trial operations.
- 21 CFR Part 50 Adaption (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19040707) - Protection of human
  subjects adapted for robotic-mediated informed consent and safety oversight.
- 21 CFR Part 312 Adaption (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19057628) - Investigational
  New Drug regulations adapted for Physical AI trial IND management.

PSL scoring follows the Physical Safety Level framework as described in the
Unification Standard Level (USL) publication (Kawchak, 2026;
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18778220). The patient journey framework
(Kawchak, 2026; DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19119939) validated single-patient
autonomous trial orchestration as a precursor to this multi-patient cycle.

## PSL Scoring Dimensions

- Dimension A (Omniscient): Measures robot awareness - sensor coverage,
  AI model depth, clinical data integration, real-time decision quality.
- Dimension B (Omnipresent): Measures robot availability - instance count,
  temporal coverage, concurrent patient capacity, geographic reach.
- Dimension C (Omnipotent): Measures robot capability - procedural range,
  precision, force control, autonomous intervention scope.
- PSL = mean(Dim A, Dim B, Dim C), rounded to 1 decimal place.
- Constraint: Maximum change of 0.3 per hour per dimension per robot type.

## Strongest and Weakest Dimensions Analysis

### Strongest Performing Dimensions

1. **RT Motion-Tracking Dim A (7.9)** - Highest individual dimension score.
   The 120 Hz marker tracking, breathing pattern AI model, and continuous
   dose accumulation awareness consistently demonstrated precision across
   26 treatment fractions involving diverse tumor sites (lung, liver, brain).
   Beam gating efficiency averaged 94.2% across all sessions.

2. **Surgical Robots Dim C (7.6)** - Second highest. The surgical fleet
   demonstrated the broadest procedural capability range in the facility,
   completing 7 surgeries spanning thoracoscopic resection, esophagectomy,
   hepatectomy, Whipple procedure, and thyroidectomy. All achieved R0
   resection with zero intraoperative complications.

3. **RT Positioning Dim A (7.5)** - Maintained its baseline of 7.5
   throughout the cycle, reflecting robust sub-millimeter positioning
   accuracy that was already well-established.

4. **Social Companion Dim B (7.4)** - Highest omnipresence score across
   all robots. The 5-instance companion fleet demonstrated 24-hour
   continuous monitoring capability, with overnight pediatric monitoring
   (P0005 carryover, P0173 Ewing sarcoma) validating temporal coverage.

### Weakest Performing Dimensions

1. **Social Companion Dim C (4.1)** - Lowest individual dimension score.
   By design, companion robots do not perform clinical procedures. Their
   omnipotence is inherently limited to interaction, anxiety management,
   and comfort provision. This is an architectural limitation, not a
   performance deficiency.

2. **Steerable Needle Dim B (5.2)** - Lowest omnipresence score among
   non-companion robots. With only 2 instances and high per-procedure
   time commitment (48 min average), the steerable needle system cannot
   demonstrate broad temporal or concurrent coverage. Expansion to 3-4
   instances would improve this dimension.

3. **Humanoids Dim C (5.3)** - Humanoid robots remain limited in
   procedural scope. Their capabilities are primarily in patient
   interaction, therapy guidance, and rehabilitation support rather
   than direct clinical intervention.

4. **Rehab Exoskeletons Dim A (5.5)** - Rehabilitation exoskeletons have
   limited clinical data awareness compared to treatment-focused robots.
   Their sensing is primarily kinematic (joint angles, force) rather
   than clinical (tumor, imaging, dosimetry).

## USL Comparison

The USL framework (Kawchak, 2026; DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18778220) evaluates
robot technical interoperability separately from the PSL clinical

| RT Positioning | 7.6 | 6.9 | -0.7 | Positioning precision not fully exercised |

| Social Companion | 5.0 | 5.7 | +0.7 | Clinical value exceeds technical sophistication |
| Humanoids | 5.4 | 5.8 | +0.4 | Clinical value exceeds technical sophistication |
| RT Motion-Tracking | 7.9 | 7.1 | -0.8 | Highest tech ceiling; clinical approaching |
| Imaging Assistant | 7.4 | 6.7 | -0.7 | High-volume imaging not fully reflected in PSL |
| Steerable Needle | 7.5 | 6.5 | -1.0 | Largest gap; low volume limits PSL demonstration |
| Rehab Exoskeletons | 5.2 | 5.6 | +0.4 | Clinical value in rehab context exceeds tech |

The average USL-PSL gap is -0.36, indicating that technical capabilities
generally exceed demonstrated clinical performance. This gap represents
the growth opportunity for future 24-hour cycles where higher patient
volume and procedure diversity can close the gap.

Notable: Social Companion, Humanoids, and Rehab Exoskeletons show positive
gaps (PSL > USL), indicating that their clinical value in patient-facing
roles exceeds their raw technical interoperability scores. This validates
the PSL framework's emphasis on clinical outcome over technical specification.

## Recommendations for Improvement

### Priority 1: Steerable Needle Expansion
- Current: 2 instances, PSL 6.5, no change over 24 hours
- Recommendation: Add 1-2 instances to improve Dim B (omnipresence)
- Target: PSL 6.8-7.0 within next multi-day cycle
- USL gap of -1.0 is the largest; more volume would close this

### Priority 2: Rehab Exoskeleton Case Volume
- Current: 2 active instances, PSL 5.6, no change over 24 hours
- Recommendation: Increase rehab case referrals from surgical and RT
  pathways. Many post-surgical patients could benefit from same-day
  robotic rehabilitation initiation.
- Target: PSL 5.8-6.0 with doubled case volume

### Priority 3: Social Companion Clinical Data Integration
- Current: Dim A 5.6 (lowest among Dim A scores for non-rehab robots)
- Recommendation: Integrate vital sign awareness and treatment schedule
  data into companion interaction model. Allow companions to detect
  early signs of distress (elevated HR, facial pain indicators) and
  alert clinical systems.
- Target: Dim A 6.0-6.5

### Priority 4: Humanoid Procedural Capability
- Current: Dim C 5.3
- Recommendation: Train humanoid robots for basic clinical tasks such
  as wound dressing changes, medication delivery, and basic vital sign
  measurement to increase their omnipotence score.
- Target: Dim C 5.8-6.0

### Priority 5: Overnight Utilization Optimization
- Current: Hours 00-05 average 5-10% utilization
- Recommendation: Expand overnight scheduling options for RT fractions,
  imaging follow-ups, and companion sessions. The on-demand model
  already supports this; marketing and patient education would increase
  overnight uptake.
- Target: 15-20% overnight utilization

## Conclusion

The 24-hour PSL trajectory demonstrates conservative, evidence-based
scoring with a cumulative site improvement of +1.0 (63.4 to 64.4). The
site maintains its Advanced classification. Eight of 10 robot types showed
measurable improvement, with Surgical Robots and Imaging Assistant showing
the largest gains (+0.2 each). Two robot types (Steerable Needle and Rehab
Exoskeletons) showed no change, primarily due to limited case volume
rather than performance deficiency. All PSL changes complied with the
0.3/hr/dim constraint. The USL-PSL comparison reveals growth opportunities
that can be addressed through instance expansion, increased case volume,
and capability enhancements in subsequent cycles.

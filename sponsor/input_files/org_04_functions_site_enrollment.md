# Sponsor Organization for Oncology Clinical Trials in 2026
## Chunk 4: Functional Responsibilities — Site Selection, Startup, Enrollment, and Monitoring

### Site Selection, Country Strategy, and Study Startup

**Initiate:** Sponsors initiate site selection through feasibility—combining investigator relationships (KOL networks), historical performance, and increasingly RWD-driven feasibility (claims/EHR/registry signals) to estimate eligible patient density and competing trials. FDA's RWD guidance on registries and on EHR/claims data supports systematic fitness evaluation when such data are used for regulatory-relevant decisions. [7]

**Operationalize/run:** Startup execution is frequently CRO-led but sponsor-governed. Sponsor functions include vendor governance, country prioritization, and ensuring regulatory/ethics submissions align with jurisdictional requirements. In the EU, CTIS drives sponsor operational needs, including structured submission workflows and transparency preparation. [51]

**Handle issues:**

Startup delays (contracts/budgets, ethics timelines): Sponsors mitigate by standard contract templates, prioritized country wave planning, and early vendor readiness. Empirical oncology data suggest startup delays can impact accrual and success; delayed activation has been associated with lower project success in cancer studies. [52]

Diversity and representativeness: Sponsors operationalize diversity goals through site geography, decentralized participation, and eligibility design. The FDA's 2025 revision guidance on enhancing participation details tactics sponsors can apply, including site geography choices and use of digital tools. [48]

System readiness: electronic systems and signatures must meet Part 11 and FDA electronic systems guidance expectations, shaping sponsor IT quality, access governance, and vendor qualification. [53]

**Finish:** Study startup "finish" is typically defined as (1) site activation readiness, (2) first-site-activated milestone, and (3) first-patient-in (FPI); sponsors formalize these as decision gates with KPI targets (cycle time, activation rate vs plan) and documented readiness reviews.

### Enrollment, Conduct, Monitoring, and Data Quality Control

**Initiate:** Enrollment begins with site activation and recruitment rollout; sponsor medical, operations, and analytics align on enrollment risk assessment and pre-specified triggers (e.g., enrollment velocity thresholds, screen failure bands).

**Operationalize/run:** 2026 execution typically uses a hybrid model:

Decentralized elements: remote visits, local HCP visits, home nursing, direct-to-patient IP logistics where appropriate, with sponsor responsibility for oversight and data integrity unchanged. [4]

Monitoring modernization: ICH E6(R3) explicitly includes centralized monitoring and data analytics as monitoring methods and recognizes roles such as data scientists in monitoring activities. [54]

RBM: FDA's RBM guidance and Q&A encourage centralized monitoring where appropriate and formal monitoring plans tied to risk. [5]

Electronic systems controls: FDA's 2024 electronic systems guidance emphasizes audit trails, data security, and validation of electronic systems used in clinical investigations—organizationally requiring sponsor-side IT quality, vendor management, and inspection readiness. [55]

**Handle issues:**

Enrollment shortfalls: Sponsors respond through site additions, broadening eligibility consistent with scientific/ethical constraints, integrating decentralized elements to reduce patient burden, and using RWD for targeted site/patient identification. [56]

Protocol deviations and data integrity risks: Sponsors operationalize ICH E6(R3)'s quality expectations through QTLs/acceptable ranges, KRIs, and CAPA governance; monitoring teams use centralized analytics to detect missingness, outliers, and unusual variability across sites. [50]

Supply/logistics disruptions: Decentralized shipping and complex oncology supply chains create failure points (temperature excursions, accountability). Sponsor recordkeeping obligations include investigational product disposition records. [57]

Inspections and audit findings: Sponsor QA and regulatory operations coordinate "inspection readiness" artifacts (system validation evidence, access logs, TMF completeness, vendor oversight evidence). ICH E6(R3) treats validated computerized systems and committee documentation as potentially essential records, underscoring why these artifacts must be inspection-ready. [58]

**Finish:** The "conduct" phase finishes with last-patient-last-visit and operational closure. A key success factor is minimizing the lag to database lock and statistical analysis readiness; industry-reported cycle times highlight the importance of streamlined data cleaning and updates. [59] (Because such benchmarks are not regulator-issued and vary widely, sponsors increasingly manage these as internal KPIs rather than "standards.")

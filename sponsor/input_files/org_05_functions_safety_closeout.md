# Sponsor Organization for Oncology Clinical Trials in 2026
## Chunk 5: Functional Responsibilities — Safety, Data/Biostatistics, Medical Affairs, and Closeout

### Safety, Pharmacovigilance, and Signal Management

**Initiate:** Safety is initiated through safety strategy, protocol safety sections, and safety monitoring committees (internal SMT; often independent DMC). FDA provides a DMC guidance framework; ICH E6(R3) emphasizes participant safety and reliable results throughout. [60]

**Operationalize/run:** Safety operations run through expedited reporting systems, case processing, medical review, aggregate review, and periodic reporting. In the U.S., IND safety reporting requirements are defined in 21 CFR 312.32. [61] Globally, ICH E2A defines standards for expedited reporting, and ICH E2B(R3) covers electronic transmission of ICSRs—both of which drive sponsor PV system design and vendor selection. [62]

**Handle issues:**

Safety signals: Sponsors escalate through medical monitor → safety governance → DMC and health authority interactions, potentially triggering protocol amendments, informed consent updates, and operational mitigation (dose holds, monitoring intensification). The organization must be able to trace decisions and maintain essential records, consistent with E6(R3) expectations for documented oversight and essential records accessibility. [63]

Decentralized safety monitoring: Decentralized elements increase reliance on DHTs and remote assessments, requiring controls for device validation/usability and data integrity as described in FDA decentralized elements guidance. [64]

**Finish:** The safety function finishes the trial with final aggregate analyses feeding the CSR, submission safety modules, and ongoing PV planning for post-approval commitments (especially common in accelerated approval pathways, though pathway specifics are outside the scope of the cited operational guidances).

### Data Management, Biostatistics, and Regulatory Submissions

**Initiate:** Data strategy begins at protocol design: CRF design, endpoint operationalization, data sources (EDC, labs, imaging, ePRO, DHTs), and standards mapping.

**Operationalize/run:** In 2026, sponsors increasingly run cloud-based EDC and eTMF ecosystems, but regulatory expectations emphasize controls rather than platform choice. FDA's electronic systems guidance and Part 11 govern data integrity, audit trails, and access controls. [6] Data standardization is central: FDA's Study Data Technical Conformance Guide and Data Standards Catalog guide how SDTM/ADaM and related artifacts should be prepared for review. [20]

**Handle issues:**

Data integrity threats: Sponsors treat system validation documentation, vendor controls, and traceability from analysis to submission datasets as inspection-critical, aligning with ICH E6(R3)'s essential record concepts (including validation records and computerised system assessment). [65]

Interim analyses / adaptive changes: Sponsors apply strict access controls and documentation for interim analysis governance, consistent with FDA adaptive design expectations. [49]

Submission risk: Data standards nonconformance can delay review; sponsors mitigate via early conformance checks, define.xml governance, and integrated data standards planning. [66]

**Finish:** The function finishes with database lock, final analyses, CSR, and regulatory submissions. ICH E6(R3) explicitly states that clinical trial reports in marketing applications should meet ICH E3 standards or be otherwise compliant with regulatory requirements. [67]

### Medical Affairs, Disclosure, Commercialization Planning, and Closeout/Archiving

**Initiate:** Medical affairs and commercial planning begin well before top-line readout, shaping evidence strategy (comparative evidence, patient-centered outcomes, RWE roadmap). RWE organizational capability is supported by FDA's RWE program framework and subsequent data-source guidance. [68]

**Operationalize/run:** Trial disclosure is now an operational discipline, not only a policy concern:

U.S. disclosure: ClinicalTrials.gov (operated by National Library of Medicine[69]) describes legal/policy-based reporting requirements; the Final Rule implementing FDAAA 801 (42 CFR Part 11) establishes registration and results reporting expectations, with a standard deadline generally within one year of primary completion for applicable clinical trials. [70]

EU disclosure: CTIS trial results expectations include summary results and layperson summaries generally within one year of trial completion (six months pediatric) and CSR submission within 30 days of marketing authorization decision when applicable. [22]

**Handle issues:**

Late disclosure, inconsistent results narratives, or missing documentation create regulatory and reputational risk. Sponsors manage these through disclosure governance (roles, workflow, quality review) integrated with eTMF and submission teams.

Inspections after completion require that essential records remain accessible. ICH E6(R3) frames TMF/essential records management and retrieval expectations, including record location tracking and computerized system validation evidence. [71]

**Finish:** Closeout is defined by (1) completed CSR/submissions package, (2) disclosure deliverables submitted (ClinicalTrials.gov/CTIS), and (3) archiving complete. EU CTR explicitly requires archiving TMF content for at least 25 years and requires sponsor appointment of archive-responsible individuals—an organizational "must have" for EU trials. [9]

# End-to-End Sponsor Playbook for Oncology Clinical Trials in 2026
## Chunk 7: Timelines, Costs, and KPIs

### Timelines and Costs

Because modality and indication are unspecified, the most defensible "typical" numbers are those reported from cross-program benchmarks; these are directional baselines and can shift substantially based on trial size, endpoints (OS vs PFS), number of countries, screening complexity, and CMC modality. [85]

**Clinical trial budget benchmarks (per protocol; means):**
Based on a Tufts Center for the Study of Drug Development[86] impact report (March/April 2022), mean actual oncology protocol budgets were approximately $14.2M (Phase I), $25.5M (Phase II), and $65.8M (Phase III); the report also provides per-enrolled-patient budget estimates and operational burden metrics (visits, data points). [R61] [87]

**Direct daily cost of trial delays.** A Tufts CSDD white paper (Aug 2024, inflation-adjusted to 2023 USD) estimates mean direct cost per day at $55,716 (Phase III), $23,737 (Phase II), $7,829 (Phase I), and $14,091 (Phase IV), with total trial duration measured from protocol approval to database lock (or primary completion date). These numbers are often used by sponsors to justify investments in SSU acceleration, enrollment infrastructure, and RBQM. [R62] [88]

### Sponsor Decision Gates with KPIs (Illustrative, Oncology 2026)

| Gate | Sponsor "Go" Criteria (examples) | Typical KPIs Monitored | Why It Matters for Approval + Market Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Development candidate selection | Target validation; translational biomarker plan; manufacturability risk acceptable | Preclinical package readiness; CMC tech transfer status | Sets feasibility of IND/CTA and later scalability [89] |
| IND/CTA "green light" | IND/CTA dossier complete; assay plan locked; first-in-human protocol + safety monitoring plan | Days to IND active (US: 30-day review window); CTA/CTIS cycle times | Delays cascade into lost time-to-market; affects competitive position [90] |
| Dose optimization readiness | Dose–exposure–response characterized; tolerability and adherence support intended use | Dose modifications; discontinuations; exposure coverage | Core to Project Optimus expectations; protects registrational risk [3] |
| Pivotal design lock | Endpoint/estimand agreed; statistical plan validated (simulations if adaptive/Bayesian); CDx path feasible | Protocol amendment rate; feasibility-to-activation conversion | Late design reversals are expensive and delay filings [91] |
| Pivotal execution control | Enrollment on track; data quality within tolerance; safety signals manageable | Enrollment rate; screen failure; missing data; KRIs/QTLs | Directly supports interpretability and inspection readiness [92] |
| Database lock + CSR | Data cleaned; audit trail complete; CSR aligned with ICH E3 | Time LPLV→DB lock; query aging; SDTM/ADaM readiness | Submission quality and review efficiency hinge on this [93] |
| Launch + postmarketing | Confirmatory commitments funded and timelines realistic; access dossier ready | Time-to-label finalization; postmarketing milestones | Drives conversion from conditional/accelerated to full approval and reimbursement [94] |

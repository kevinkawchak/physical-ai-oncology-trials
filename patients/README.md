## Overview

This directory documents the **10-page patient-facing instructional PDF** titled *Patient Instructions: Physical AI Oncology Trials*. Each page is a self-contained instruction sheet for one robot type, designed so that an upcoming patient can visualize, read, and feel comfortable regarding how to correctly interact with a specific robot during a physical AI oncology clinical trial.

The paper covers 10 robot types across surgical, therapeutic, diagnostic, assistive, and rehabilitative categories. Each page pairs one robot type with a specific cancer type and provides a 1-sentence introduction followed by 3 clear numbered instructions covering arrival, interaction, and conclusion. Pages 5 and 6 feature pediatric patients matched to child-appropriate robots.

## Page-by-Page Content

### Page 1 — Surgical Robots 

**Introduction:** A surgical robot is a large, multi-armed machine that your doctor controls from a console nearby. It will not move on its own — every action is guided by your surgeon.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you will lie down on the operating table, and the care team will help you get comfortable. The robot arms will be positioned near your body, but they will not touch you until your surgeon is ready. |
| **2. During** | During the procedure, the robot's small instruments will work inside your body through tiny incisions (about 1–2 cm each). You will be under anesthesia and will not feel anything. The procedure may take 90–180 minutes depending on complexity. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After surgery, the robot is moved away, and you'll be taken to recovery. Expect to spend 1–2 days in the hospital. Small bandages will cover your incisions, and your care team will give you instructions for healing at home. |

**Source:** [Intuitive Surgical](https://www.intuitivesurgical.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a multi-armed surgical robot system (da Vinci-style)

---

### Page 2 — Cobots / Collaborative Robots 

**Introduction:** A cobot is a lightweight robotic arm designed to work alongside your healthcare team. It assists with precise, repetitive tasks like tissue sample handling or guiding a biopsy needle.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you'll sit or lie down near the cobot. A technician will position the arm near the area being examined. You may feel a slight sensation when the arm adjusts near your skin. |
| **2. During** | During the interaction, the cobot will move slowly and gently, assisting with tasks like guiding a biopsy needle to the correct location. The force it applies is limited to very low levels (under 5 N) and it will stop immediately if resistance is detected. The process typically takes 10–25 minutes. |
| **3. Conclusion** | When the procedure is done, the cobot retracts, and a small bandage may be placed on the biopsy site. You can usually return to normal activities the same day. |

**Source:** [Franka Robotics](https://franka.de/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a single collaborative robot arm on a stand

---

### Page 3 — RT Positioning Robots 

**Introduction:** A radiotherapy positioning robot is a robotic system that helps align your body precisely for radiation treatment. It works by adjusting the treatment table and your position automatically to target the tumor while avoiding healthy tissue.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you'll lie on the treatment table and the team will help you into the right position. The robot will make fine adjustments to align your body, using imaging to guide it. You may feel the table shift slightly. |
| **2. During** | During treatment, the robot keeps you precisely aligned while the radiation is delivered. You won't feel the radiation. The entire session usually lasts 15–30 minutes. Stay as still as you can, and the robot will do the rest. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After the session, the table returns to its starting position and you can get up. No recovery time is needed. Some patients experience mild fatigue after several sessions. |

**Source:** [Accuray](https://www.accuray.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a radiation therapy positioning system (CyberKnife-style)

---

### Page 4 — Needle-Placement Robots 

**Introduction:** A robotic needle-placement system uses a robotic arm and real-time imaging to guide a needle precisely into a tumor for biopsy or treatment. It improves accuracy and reduces the number of needle insertions needed.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you'll lie on an imaging table (CT or ultrasound). The doctor will mark the target area and the robot will be positioned nearby. You may receive local anesthesia to numb the area. |
| **2. During** | During the procedure, the robot guides a thin needle to the exact target inside your body using live imaging. You may feel pressure but should not feel sharp pain. The process typically takes 20–45 minutes and involves only 1–2 needle insertions. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After the needle is removed, a small bandage is placed on the entry point. You'll be monitored for 1–2 hours and can usually go home the same day. Avoid heavy lifting for 24–48 hours. |

**Source:** ISO 15223-1 | **Image:** Illustration featuring an ISO medical device symbol with needle-placement guidance

---

### Page 5 — Companion Robots 

**Introduction:** A companion robot is a friendly, interactive robot that talks, plays games, and provides emotional support. It is designed to help children feel more comfortable and less anxious during hospital visits.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you meet the robot, it will greet you by name and may wave or make a friendly gesture. You can talk to it, and it will respond to your voice. A nurse or child life specialist will be nearby. |
| **2. During** | During your visit, you can play games, listen to stories, or practice breathing exercises with the robot. Sessions usually last 10–20 minutes. The robot moves gently and never touches you unless you high-five it. |
| **3. Conclusion** | When it's time to go, the robot will say goodbye and may remind you about your next visit. There's nothing to remove or recover from — it's just like having a friendly conversation. |

**Source:** [SoftBank Robotics](https://www.softbankrobotics.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a friendly social companion robot (Pepper-style)

---

### Page 6 — Humanoids 

**Introduction:** A humanoid robot looks similar to a person and can walk, carry items, and interact using gestures and speech. In a hospital, it may deliver supplies, guide you to your appointment, or provide encouragement.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you first see the humanoid, it may walk toward you and introduce itself. It moves at a slow walking pace (about 1 m/s) and will keep a comfortable distance (at least 1 meter away). |
| **2. During** | During your interaction, the robot may hand you a comfort item, guide you down a hallway, or lead an activity in a playroom. Sessions typically last 15–25 minutes. It will not touch you unless you initiate a handshake or high-five. |
| **3. Conclusion** | When the visit ends, the robot will say goodbye and return to its station. No cleanup or recovery is needed. |

**Source:** [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a bipedal humanoid robot (Atlas-style)

---

### Page 7 — RT Motion-Tracking Robots 

**Introduction:** A motion-tracking robot monitors your breathing and body movements in real time during radiation treatment. It adjusts the radiation beam so that it always targets the tumor, even as you breathe.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, the team will place small markers or use a surface-tracking camera to monitor your movement. You'll lie on the treatment table and be asked to breathe normally. |
| **2. During** | During treatment, the system tracks your breathing pattern and adjusts the radiation beam in real time. You won't feel anything. The session typically takes 10–20 minutes. Just relax and breathe naturally. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After treatment, the markers (if used) are removed, and you can leave immediately. No recovery time is needed. |

**Source:** [Varian Medical](https://www.varian.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a radiation therapy motion-tracking system

---

### Page 8 — Imaging Robots 

**Introduction:** An imaging assistant robot holds and positions an ultrasound probe or imaging device with steady precision. It helps the doctor get clearer images by keeping the device perfectly still and at the correct angle.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you'll sit or lie down and the robot arm will be brought close to your neck. A small amount of gel will be applied to your skin for the ultrasound. |
| **2. During** | During the scan, the robot holds the probe steady against your skin with gentle, constant pressure (about 2–5 N). You may feel light pressure but no pain. The scan typically takes 10–20 minutes. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After the scan, the robot retracts and the gel is wiped off. You can go about your day immediately — no recovery time is needed. |

**Source:** ISO 20417 | **Image:** Illustration featuring an ISO medical labeling symbol with imaging guidance

---

### Page 9 — Steerable Needle Robots 

**Introduction:** A steerable needle robot uses a flexible, computer-guided needle that can bend around obstacles inside your body to reach a tumor. It provides more accurate targeting than a straight needle.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you arrive, you'll lie on an imaging table and the target area will be numbed with local anesthesia. The robot is positioned near the entry point on your skin. |
| **2. During** | During the procedure, the needle is inserted slowly and steered using real-time imaging (MRI or CT). You may feel pressure or a dull ache, but the anesthesia minimizes discomfort. The process usually takes 30–60 minutes. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After the needle is removed, a bandage is applied and you'll rest for 1–2 hours. Most patients go home the same day. Avoid strenuous activity for 48 hours. |

**Source:** ISO 7010 | **Image:** Illustration featuring an ISO safety symbol with steerable needle guidance

---

### Page 10 — Rehab Exoskeletons 

**Introduction:** A rehabilitation exoskeleton is a wearable robot that attaches to your legs and helps you stand and walk during physical therapy. It supports your weight and guides your leg movements.

| Step | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Arrival** | When you start a session, a physical therapist will strap the exoskeleton onto your legs and torso using adjustable supports. This typically takes 5–10 minutes. The device is lightweight (about 20 kg) and the therapist will stay by your side. |
| **2. During** | During therapy, the exoskeleton will gently move your legs through a natural walking pattern. You'll walk on a treadmill or flat surface for 15–30 minutes. The robot adjusts its support based on your strength — as you get stronger, it assists less. |
| **3. Conclusion** | After the session, the therapist will help remove the exoskeleton. You may feel tired in your legs — this is normal. Sessions typically occur 3–5 times per week. |

**Source:** [Ekso Bionics](https://eksobionics.com/) | **Image:** Colorful illustration of a lower-body rehabilitation exoskeleton

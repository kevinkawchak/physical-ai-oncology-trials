% ======================================================================
% SECTION 4: HR 9504 - Physical AI Clinical Error Reduction Act of 2026
% Adaption of HTI-1 DSI Final Rule 2023 and FDA AI Decision-Making
% Draft Guidance 2025.
% ======================================================================

HR 9504 is an \textit{Adaption of the HTI-1 DSI Final Rule of 2023
and the FDA AI Regulatory Decision-Making Draft Guidance of January
2025}, drawing the chain of prior authority from the ONC algorithm-
transparency rule through the FDA capability-neutrality draft
\cite{rank9_onc_hti1_2023, hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023,
fda_ai_regulatory_decision_draft_2025}. The bill implements the
sixth dimension of the operational definition of patient control
introduced by this paper: AI-exclusion contestability with mandatory
appeal rights, supplemented by the new error-rate publication
requirement that converts HTI-1 DSI transparency into actionable
patient-side substitution rights. The bill addresses the project
brief's central aim of reducing human doctor and nurse error rates
and the lack of physical and intellectual capabilities of human-only
care, while preserving irreplaceable human roles in empathy, consent
counseling, and ethical oversight.

\subsection{Findings and Declarations}

(a) Human doctors and nurses make errors at documented per-task
rates published in peer-reviewed literature; the bill cites the
prior published rates rather than inflating them. The literature
covers surgical site infection, medication reconciliation, needle
placement deviation, RT positioning, sepsis recognition, and
adverse-event detection from electronic patient-reported outcomes
\cite{rocque2025remote_symptom_monitoring}.

(b) AI and robotics can reduce specific error categories where
prospectively validated. The SHIELD-RT prospective randomized
controlled trial reported a 45 percent reduction in acute-care events
and a 48 percent reduction in associated costs in radiotherapy
adverse-event prediction
\cite{rocque2025remote_symptom_monitoring}. The ASyMS and eRAPID
ePRO systems demonstrated patient-side adverse-event self-management
with documented quality-of-life improvements
\cite{kearney2009asyms, maguire2017esmart, absolom2021erapid}.

(c) Computational pathology foundation models reach clinical-grade
performance across pan-cancer detection. Virchow (Nature Medicine
2024) demonstrated this performance across both common and rare
cancers, providing the technical baseline for AI second-opinion
substitution \cite{vorontsov2024virchow}.

(d) The Mazor 2025 randomized trial of AI notifications alone
returned a null result on the trial-enrollment endpoint, indicating
that AI alerts in isolation do not improve participation; the
finding informs the bill's "no automated-only denial" guardrail
\cite{mazor2025ai_notifications_trial}.

(e) Bayesian urgent-care prediction (Gonzalez 2025) and the four-
simulation accelerated patient-prediction paper at
\texttt{new-trial/national-24-7-trial/paper/full-paper/} provide
domain-specific evidence that AI patient prediction outperforms
narrow supervised models on the specific tasks tested
\cite{gonzalez2025bayesian_urgent_care_nsclc, kawchak_2026_19994945}.

(f) Humans retain irreplaceable roles in empathy, consent
counseling, and ethical oversight. The bill does not eliminate human
doctors or nurses; it shifts the patient's default for procedurally
definable tasks to physical-AI execution where the patient prefers
and the evidence supports.

(g) The FDA Real-Time Clinical Trials press announcement of April
28, 2026 establishes a regulatory opening that aligns sponsor-side
real-time signal sharing with the patient-side error-rate
substitution rights enacted by this bill \cite{fda_rtct_press_2026}.

\subsection{Definitions}

(a) \billterm{Documented human error rate} means the per-task error
rate published in peer-reviewed literature for the specific task
category (surgical site infection, medication reconciliation,
needle placement deviation, RT positioning, sepsis recognition,
adverse-event detection from ePRO).

(b) \billterm{Physical AI substitute} means a Physical AI system,
robot, or agent that can perform the same task category with a
documented error rate at or below the human baseline. The 53 core
agents at \texttt{sponsor/final\_paper/scripts/core\_agents/} (in
particular \texttt{safety\_agent.py},
\texttt{clinical\_accountability\_agent.py}, and
\texttt{audit\_trail\_manager.py}) provide the operational baseline
\cite{kawchak_2026_19396256}.

(c) \billterm{Patient-initiated AI second opinion} means the
patient's right to request, before any consequential clinical
decision, a parallel evaluation by a qualified AI system meeting
HTI-1 DSI transparency requirements \cite{hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023}.

\subsection{Error Rate Reduction Rights}

(a) Right to a published per-site, per-task human error rate prior
to consenting to any procedure.

(b) Right to substitute a Physical AI executor for any task where
the documented Physical AI error rate is below the human baseline
and the patient prefers AI execution.

(c) Right to a patient-initiated AI second opinion, generated by a
qualified system, prior to any consequential clinical decision
(surgery, chemotherapy regimen change, end-of-life care decision)
\cite{hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023}.

(d) Right to mandatory error tracking across all sites, with public
per-site reporting. The dashboard at
\texttt{sponsor/final\_paper/scripts/dashboard/} and the 168-hour
cumulative metrics at
\texttt{sponsor/final\_paper/168\_hours/instructions/core\_i5\_6200u\_4gb/sponsor\_168h\_summary.json}
provide the operational reporting pattern
\cite{kawchak_2026_19396256}.

(e) Right to refuse a human-only execution path for any task where
a qualified Physical AI substitute exists, mirroring the FDA RTCT
pilot's "real-time signal sharing" model \cite{fda_rtct_press_2026}.

(f) Right to AI-exclusion contestability: any patient excluded from
a trial, treatment, or procedural pathway by an automated decision
may appeal under the HTI-1 DSI transparency framework
\cite{rank9_onc_hti1_2023, hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023}.

\subsection{Implementation}

(a) The Secretary, acting through the Food and Drug Administration,
the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, the Office of the
National Coordinator for Health Information Technology, and the
Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, shall require all CMS-
billable sites to publish per-task human and AI error rates within
18 months of enactment.

(b) HTI-1 DSI transparency requirements apply to every AI second-
opinion system deployed under Subsection 4.3(c)
\cite{hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023}.

(c) The patient priority operational baseline at
\texttt{new-trial/national-24-7-trial/paper/full-paper/sections/discussion.tex}
informs the implementation guidance \cite{kawchak_2026_19994945}.

\subsection{Reporting and Enforcement}

(a) Annual federal error-rate report by site, by task, and by AI
substitute (where one is deployed).

(b) CMS reimbursement penalty for sites that fail to publish error
rates by the 18-month deadline.

(c) Information-blocking penalty harmonized with the Cures Act
disincentives rule \cite{federal_register_info_blocking_disincentives_2024}.

(d) The federal Patient Control Dashboard's Domain 3 (Control-in-
Action) and Domain 4 (Fairness and Technical Quality) anchor the
report. Domain 3 metrics: hospitalization rate, urgent-care or ED
use, alert response time, false-alert burden, serious adverse events
in home or robotics settings. Domain 4 metrics: declared context of
use, external validation, subgroup performance, calibration
monitoring, update-control disclosure, revalidation after model
changes \cite{rocque2025remote_symptom_monitoring,
gonzalez2025bayesian_urgent_care_nsclc,
dronca2026cancer_care_beyond_walls, target2025robotic_bronchoscopy,
fda_ai_regulatory_decision_draft_2025, vorontsov2024virchow}.

\subsection{Implementation Guardrails}

The bill is bound by three guardrails that anchor the bills paper-
wide and that are restated in HR 9506 for the cross-bill leadership
authority.

(i) No patient denied trial consideration by automated output
alone.

(ii) Capability-neutral statutory rights that follow any AI
architecture (LLM, foundation model, agentic system, robotics
platform), drawing on the FDA AI Regulatory Decision-Making Draft
\cite{fda_ai_regulatory_decision_draft_2025}.

(iii) Mandatory annual public reporting, auditability, and sunset
review by Congress under FDORA Section 3209 authorities
\cite{rank4_fdora_fda_modernization_2022}.

\subsection{Discussion and Limitation}

The bill extends ONC HTI-1 DSI transparency into mandatory error-
rate publication and patient-initiated AI second-opinion rights. The
ePRO and remote symptom monitoring evidence (Rocque 2025, ASyMS,
eRAPID) supports the bill without overclaiming. The Mazor 2025 null
result on AI notifications alone is critical and is incorporated
into the "no automated-only denial" guardrail
\cite{rank9_onc_hti1_2023, rocque2025remote_symptom_monitoring,
kearney2009asyms, maguire2017esmart, absolom2021erapid,
mazor2025ai_notifications_trial}. The bill is the most contentious
of the seven because it touches workforce questions beyond the
narrow project brief; the Huang 2025 null result indicates that
machine learning does not always exceed Cox regression on real-
world structured survival data; SHIELD-RT is convincing for
radiotherapy but does not generalize without prospective validation
in each domain. The bill respects human doctor and nurse roles in
empathy, consent counseling, and ethical oversight, and constrains
the AI-substitution path to procedurally definable tasks with
prospectively validated error reductions \cite{kawchak_2026_19994945,
kawchak_2026_19119939}.

\subsection{Patient-Control Framings}

For the individual patient, a 58-year-old female NSCLC patient
matching PAT-2026-0042 reviews the published per-site error rates
at her chosen site, requests an AI second opinion before her cycle-1
pembrolizumab dosing decision, and refuses a human-only RT
positioning step in favor of the RTPOS-01 robot documented at hour-12
of \texttt{new-trial/national-24-7-trial/hour-12/robot\_logs.md}
\cite{kawchak_2026_19994945, kawchak_2026_19119939}.

For broad adoption, all United States CMS-billable oncology trial
sites must publish per-task error rates within 18 months. The bill
positions the United States as Number 1 in the world for evidence-
based patient-led error reduction. The bill explicitly respects the
human doctor and nurse roles that remain irreplaceable (empathy,
consent counseling, ethical oversight) and constrains the AI-
substitution path to procedurally definable tasks with prospectively
validated error reductions \cite{kawchak_2026_19994945,
kawchak_2026_19119939}.

% ======================================================================
% SECTION 3: HR 9503 - Cancer Patient Procedural Modification
% Authority Act of 2026
% Adaption of OHRP Broad Consent 2017 and 21st Century Cures Act 2016.
% ======================================================================

HR 9503 is an \textit{Adaption of OHRP Broad Consent Recommendations
2017 and the 21st Century Cures Act of 2016 (PL 114-255)}, drawing
the chain of prior authority from the Common Rule baseline at 45
CFR 46 through the federal broad-consent recommendations and the
Cures Act information-blocking statute that already binds
interoperable health information technology
\cite{ohrp_broad_consent_attachment_c_2017, rank1_cures_act_2016,
hhs_45cfr46_protection_human_subjects}. The bill implements the
second dimension of the operational definition of patient control:
enrollment comprehension, key information rules, broad consent, and
patient-initiated translation of plain-language summaries with real-
time consent updates \cite{fda_informed_consent_guidance_2023,
fda_ohrp_key_information_draft_2024}.

The current legal stack supplies the foundation for HR 9503. FDA
Informed Consent Guidance 2023, the FDA / OHRP Key Information Draft
of 2024, and the OHRP Broad Consent Recommendations of 2017 establish
the consent and broad-consent frameworks. HR 9503 closes the patient-
control gap by adding a positive procedural-modification right where
the patient may modify the patient's own treatment under physical-AI
execution, governed by a real-time consent update logged within
five minutes of the modification \cite{fda_informed_consent_guidance_2023,
fda_ohrp_key_information_draft_2024,
ohrp_broad_consent_attachment_c_2017}.

\subsection{Findings and Declarations}

(a) The current consent framework permits broad consent for secondary
biospecimen use under the Common Rule, but procedure-level consent
for the index trial remains binary: accept the entire protocol or
refuse \cite{hhs_45cfr46_protection_human_subjects,
ohrp_broad_consent_attachment_c_2017}.

(b) Physical AI executors with USL scores at or above the
modification-tier threshold can perform per-cycle and per-step patient-
initiated modifications with no documented loss of safety. The 35
pembrolizumab cycles managed by Franka Emika at USL 88.75 in
\texttt{patient-journey/stage\_07\_immunotherapy.py} provide a worked
example \cite{kawchak_2026_19119939, kawchak_2026_18778219}.

(c) The ICH E6(R3) adaption at
\texttt{national-platform/ich\_e6r3\_adapt/01\_preamble\_principles\_investigator.tex}
and \texttt{02\_sponsor\_responsibilities.tex} already permits real-
time consent updates as a recognized form of investigator-sponsor
correspondence; the bill formalizes the patient-initiated form
\cite{kawchak_2026_18973368}.

(d) The 21 CFR Part 50 adaption at
\texttt{regulatory/} provides the Common Rule informed-consent
language that HR 9503 builds on \cite{kawchak_2026_19040707}.

(e) Information-blocking penalties under the federal register
disincentives rule provide the enforcement teeth for the bill's
five-minute logging requirement
\cite{federal_register_info_blocking_disincentives_2024}.

\subsection{Definitions}

(a) \billterm{Procedural modification} means a patient-initiated
change to a pre-approved protocol step, executed by the qualified
Physical AI system, that does not alter the primary endpoint or the
safety profile beyond pre-defined tolerances.

(b) \billterm{Real-time consent update} means a HIPAA-compliant,
FHIR-based consent revision logged in the patient's electronic health
record within five minutes of the modification. The FHIR
infrastructure baseline is the MCP-PAI five-server topology with hash-
chained audit trails at
\texttt{national-platform/national\_mcp/chunk2\_methods\_and\_results\_part1.tex}
and \texttt{chunk3\_results\_part2.tex} \cite{kawchak_2026_19244918}.

(c) \billterm{Qualified Physical AI executor} means a robot or
robotic system with a USL score above the modification-tier
threshold (USL 8.0 or above for surgical modifications, 6.0 or above
for therapeutic positioning modifications, 4.0 or above for
rehabilitation modifications) \cite{kawchak_2026_18778219}.

(d) \billterm{Modifiable steps matrix} means a per-protocol matrix
listing every step that the patient may modify under broad consent
without requiring fresh IRB review. The matrix is delivered as part
of the trial-enrollment package and is updated whenever the protocol
itself is amended.

\subsection{Patient Procedural Modification Rights}

(a) Right to request, at any point during the trial, a modification
to a pre-approved procedural step. The qualified Physical AI executor
evaluates safety in real time and either executes or declines the
modification with a cited reason logged in the patient's record.

(b) Right to receive, in plain language, the safety evaluation
result for any requested modification within five minutes. If the
modification is declined, the patient may appeal to the IRB on call.
The IRB framework at
\texttt{national-platform/21cfr50\_adapt/02\_irb\_review\_pediatric.tex}
applies \cite{kawchak_2026_19040707}.

(c) Right to revise broad consent at any point with 24-hour notice,
drawing on the OHRP broad-consent baseline
\cite{ohrp_broad_consent_attachment_c_2017}.

(d) Right to specify, before each procedure, which steps may be
modified by the patient and which steps require investigator review.
The 10-stage patient journey at \texttt{patient-journey/} enumerates
the per-stage modification points across
\texttt{stage\_05\_surgery.py} through
\texttt{stage\_10\_closeout.py} \cite{kawchak_2026_19119939}.

(e) Right to request that a procedural step be performed by a
Physical AI system rather than by a human doctor or nurse where doing
so reduces the documented error rate. This right dovetails with HR
9504 and is enforceable through that bill's error-rate publication
requirement.

(f) Right to a per-cycle modifiable steps matrix delivered as part
of the trial enrollment package, listing every step that the patient
may modify under broad consent without requiring fresh IRB review.

\subsection{Implementation}

(a) The Secretary, acting through the Food and Drug Administration,
the Office for Human Research Protections, and the Department of
Health and Human Services, shall require federal IRB networks to
publish a per-protocol modifiable-steps matrix within 18 months of
enactment.

(b) The California baseline at
\texttt{new-trial/site/02-legislation-patient-rights/physical\_ai\_patient\_rights.tex}
and at
\texttt{national-platform/new\_trial\_psl/02\_ab2847\_patient\_rights.tex}
is preserved by the preemption rule \cite{kawchak_2026_19176370}.

(c) The MCP-PAI infrastructure required by Subsection 3.2(b) shall
be deployed at all CMS-billable Physical AI oncology trial sites
within 24 months of enactment \cite{kawchak_2026_19244918}.

\subsection{Reporting and Enforcement}

(a) Annual federal report on modification rate by stage, by cancer,
by robot category. Domain 2 of the federal Patient Control Dashboard
(Decision Quality) anchors this report: consent comprehension,
decisional-conflict scores, goal-trial concordance, AI-exclusion
appeal success rate, and explanation and provenance coverage. The
HTI-1 DSI transparency framework provides the technical baseline for
each metric \cite{hti1_dsi_fact_sheet_2023}.

(b) The audit-trail manager at
\texttt{sponsor/final\_paper/scripts/core\_agents/audit\_trail\_manager.py}
provides the operational baseline for hash-chained provenance of
every modification request \cite{kawchak_2026_19396256}.

(c) Civil penalties harmonize with 45 CFR 46 enforcement and the
Cures Act information-blocking framework
\cite{federal_register_info_blocking_disincentives_2024}.

\subsection{Discussion and Limitation}

The bill extends the prior 21st Century Cures Act broad-consent
permissions into a positive procedural modification authority and
extends the OHRP Broad Consent Recommendations 2017 into a real-
time consent update framework \cite{rank1_cures_act_2016,
onc_cures_act_final_rule_page, ohrp_broad_consent_attachment_c_2017}.
The principal limitation is that the five-minute consent-update
logging requirement assumes MCP-PAI infrastructure that is documented
but not yet field-deployed at scale; the modifiable-steps matrix
presumes IRB review at scale that has not been tested in production
oncology trials \cite{kawchak_2026_18973368}.

\subsection{Patient-Control Framings}

For the individual patient, a 58-year-old female NSCLC patient
matching PAT-2026-0042 in
\texttt{patient-journey/stage\_07\_immunotherapy.py} requests at
cycle 22 of 35 pembrolizumab that the Franka Emika cobot deliver the
infusion 30 minutes earlier than the protocol default to accommodate
work, with the qualified Physical AI executor evaluating safety in
4 minutes and approving the modification under the patient's broad-
consent revision \cite{kawchak_2026_19119939, kawchak_2026_18778219}.

For broad adoption, all Physical AI oncology trial sites must
publish a modifiable-steps matrix per protocol within 18 months. The
bill positions the United States as Number 1 in the world for
patient-led procedural modification under physical-AI execution,
replacing the prior all-or-nothing consent pattern under traditional
IRB review with a granular per-step national standard
\cite{kawchak_2026_18973368, kawchak_2026_19119939}.

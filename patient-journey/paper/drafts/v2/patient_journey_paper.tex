\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a comprehensive single-patient journey through a fully regulated Physical AI oncology clinical trial illustration, tracing Patient PAT-2026-0042---a 58-year-old female diagnosed with Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)---across ten sequential stages from pre-screening referral through trial closeout. The journey integrates robotic-assisted surgery using the da Vinci Xi surgical robot (Unification Standard Level 7.9/10), patient-specific digital twin modeling with four calibrated tumor growth models validated under the ASME V\&V 40 framework, federated learning across five clinical sites with differential privacy ($\varepsilon$=1.0, $\delta$=10$^{-5}$), and 35 cycles of adjuvant pembrolizumab immunotherapy. The entire project was conducted as a fully autonomous AI workflow: Claude Code Opus 4.6 executed 13 commits across 72 minutes in a single pull request without user intervention, producing 13 Python orchestrator modules comprising approximately 7,860 lines of code, 30 ASCII progress diagrams across three perspectives per stage, and a comprehensive deliverables package including six summary tables, six architectural diagrams, four stakeholder guidance documents, and an FDA cost-savings analysis projecting 30--50\% total trial cost reductions (\$390M--\$650M against a \$1.3B baseline). Regulatory compliance spans 84 sections across 21 CFR Part 312 (including the adapted Subpart J for Physical AI systems), 21 CFR Part 50 (including the adapted Subpart C), and ICH E6(R3), with each Python script accessible in real time via the branch and each text diagram output viewable for relevancy, correctness, and timestamp-based monitoring---providing credibility and explainability to the autonomous results. The patient achieved a complete response (CR) with 36-month event-free survival, zero device-related adverse events, and a federated hazard ratio of 0.62 (95\% CrI: 0.44--0.87) favoring the experimental Physical AI arm. This work is published as a patient journey based on three Physical AI regulatory adaptations conducted by the author and is not to be considered a new and approved regulatory paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%% ========================================================================

\vspace{-0.2cm}

The convergence of autonomous robotic systems, patient-specific digital twins, and federated machine learning has created an unprecedented opportunity to transform oncology clinical trials from fragmented, site-specific operations into integrated, AI-augmented workflows governed by rigorous regulatory frameworks. This paper documents the complete journey of a single cancer patient---PAT-2026-0042, a 58-year-old female with Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 1, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) tumor proportion score of 65\%, and tumor mutational burden (TMB) of 14 mutations per megabase---through a ten-stage Physical AI oncology trial illustration that spans from initial referral through final data lock and archival.

\vspace{-0.1cm}

The patient journey described here was built entirely within the \texttt{physical-ai-oncology-trials} repository (v2.6.0), which at the time of this work contained 51 Python modules totaling 40,526 lines of code, 262 tests across 54 test modules, and three prior regulatory adaptations: an adaptation of ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice guidance for Physical AI systems (v2.2.0), an adaptation of 21 CFR Part 50 with a new Subpart C addressing additional protections for Physical AI investigations (v2.4.0), and an adaptation of 21 CFR Part 312 with a new Subpart J governing Physical AI systems in clinical investigations (v2.5.0). These three regulatory adaptations---each produced as LaTeX documents with comprehensive bibliographies---established the legal and procedural foundation upon which the patient journey operates.

What distinguishes this project is the fully autonomous nature of its execution. Claude Code Opus 4.6 ran the entire single-patient journey workflow from a single prompt (v2.6.0, documented in \texttt{patient-journey/prompts.md}), producing 13 sequential commits over 72 minutes in one pull request (PR \#41) without any user intervention. Each commit created new files---Python orchestrator modules, patient state models, ASCII progress diagrams, deliverable tables, and FDA cost-savings analyses---that were immediately accessible in the branch for real-time monitoring. The 30 text diagram outputs across three perspectives (timeline, regulatory, and clinical) for each of the ten stages provided human-readable, timestamp-verifiable artifacts that allowed the user to assess relevancy, correctness, and progression without interrupting the autonomous workflow.

\vspace{-0.1cm}

This combination of autonomous execution with transparent, monitorable intermediate outputs represents a new paradigm for AI-assisted clinical research tooling: the AI system performs end-to-end generation while the human retains full observability and the ability to intervene at any commit boundary.

\subsection{Regulatory Foundation}
\label{sec:regulatory-foundation}
%% ========================================================================

The patient journey rests upon three adapted regulatory frameworks that were developed in prior releases of the \texttt{physical-ai-oncology-trials} repository. Each adaptation extended existing FDA and ICH regulations to address the unique requirements of Physical AI systems---robotic surgical platforms, digital twin engines, and federated learning networks---operating within clinical investigations.

The first adaptation (v2.2.0) addressed ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice, extending Sections 1 through 4 with Physical AI-specific provisions. Section 1.4 introduced digital twin validation requirements under the ASME V\&V 40 framework, Section 1.5 established the Unification Standard Level (USL) scoring methodology for robot qualification, and Section 2.8 defined six Physical AI consent elements that must be disclosed to trial participants. The adaptation included Appendices A through C and a 30-definition glossary referencing all nine USL-evaluated robots across three categories: surgical robots (da Vinci Xi 7.1, Hugo RAS 4.5, Versius 3.4), collaborative robots (Franka Panda 7.4, Kinova Gen3 5.7, xArm 7 3.4), and humanoid robots (Atlas 5.8, Digit 4.2, Optimus 3.6).

The second adaptation (v2.4.0) extended 21 CFR Part 50---Protection of Human Subjects---by introducing a new Subpart C: Additional Protections for Physical AI Investigations. This subpart established \S50.30 (safety requirements for Physical AI systems during clinical procedures), \S50.31 (IRB review requirements for Physical AI protocols), \S50.32 (ongoing consent and participant notification obligations), \S50.33 (investigator notification and data protection requirements), and \S50.34 (emergency provisions for Physical AI system failures). The 37-page document included 17 new Physical AI definitions and 19 bibliography references.

The third adaptation (v2.5.0) extended 21 CFR Part 312---Investigational New Drug Applications---by introducing a new Subpart J: Physical AI Systems in Clinical Investigations. This 94-page document added five new sections: \S312.401 (three-tier Physical AI classification), \S312.402 (validation requirements including USL scoring), \S312.403 (cybersecurity standards including TLS 1.3, MFA, and SBOM requirements), \S312.404 (human oversight with autonomy levels 0--4), and \S312.405 (lifecycle management per IEC 62304). The adaptation included 21 new Physical AI definitions and 42 bibliography references.

Together, these three adaptations provide 84+ regulatory sections that govern every aspect of the patient journey, from initial PHI scanning under HIPAA Safe Harbor (\S50.33) through final data lock with 21 CFR Part 11 electronic signatures (\S312.57).

\subsection{The Patient}
\label{sec:the-patient}
%% ========================================================================

Patient PAT-2026-0042 is a 58-year-old female former smoker diagnosed with Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma histology) at SITE-003. Her baseline characteristics---defined in \texttt{patient\_state.py} using 25 dataclasses and 10 enumerations---include an ECOG performance status of 1, PD-L1 TPS of 65\%, TMB of 14 mut/Mb, tumor volume of 42.3 cm$^3$ with a growth rate of 0.023/day, adequate organ function (eGFR 92 mL/min, cardiac ejection fraction 62\%, FEV1 78\% predicted), and negative biomarkers for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, KRAS, BRAF, HER2, and NTRK alterations. This molecular profile---high PD-L1 expression without actionable driver mutations---makes her an ideal candidate for pembrolizumab-based immunotherapy combined with robotic-assisted surgical resection.

\subsection{Development Workflow}
\label{sec:development-workflow}

The patient journey was developed using a fully autonomous AI workflow powered by Claude Code Opus 4.6. The development prompt (v2.6.0) was documented in \texttt{patient-journey/prompts.md} and specified 12 sequential commits, each building on prior work without overwriting previously committed files. The prompt defined the patient demographics (PAT-2026-0042, 58F, Stage IIIB NSCLC), required three text-based progress diagrams per stage, and mandated comprehensive deliverables including tables, charts, guidance documents, and FDA cost-savings analysis.

The autonomous workflow produced the following artifacts across 13 commits in 72 minutes via a single pull request (PR \#41):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Commits 1--10: Stage orchestrators.} Each commit created a Python module implementing one of the ten journey stages (\texttt{stage\_01\_prescreening.py} through \texttt{stage\_10\_closeout.py}), along with the central patient state model (\texttt{patient\_state.py}, 545 lines, Commit 1) and the master journey orchestrator (\texttt{master\_journey.py}, 792 lines, Commit 10). Each stage commit also produced three ASCII progress diagrams in \texttt{patient-journey/diagrams/stage\_XX/}.

\item \textbf{Commit 11: Verification and cross-stage validation.} Added comprehensive test infrastructure and cross-stage consistency checks to validate state propagation across all ten stages.

\item \textbf{Commit 12: Deliverables package.} Generated six summary tables in \texttt{deliverables/tables/}, six architectural diagrams in \texttt{deliverables/diagrams/}, ten Plotly chart generators in \texttt{deliverables/charts/}, four stakeholder guidance documents in \texttt{deliverables/guidance/}, and the FDA cost-savings analysis in \texttt{deliverables/fda\_cost\_savings/}.

\item \textbf{Commit 13: Repository updates.} Updated \texttt{README.md}, \texttt{releases.md}, \texttt{CHANGELOG.md}, and \texttt{prompts.md} for the v2.6.0 release with complete documentation of the patient journey infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Software Architecture}
\label{sec:software-architecture}

The patient journey is implemented as a layered Python architecture within the \texttt{patient-journey/} directory:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Data layer} (\texttt{patient\_state.py}, 545 lines): Defines 25 dataclasses (including \texttt{PatientDemographics}, \texttt{TumorProfile}, \texttt{Biomarkers}, \texttt{DigitalTwinState}, \texttt{RobotQualification}, \texttt{FederationContribution}, \texttt{RegulatoryEvent}, \texttt{ConsentRecord}, and \texttt{AuditEntry}) and 10 enumerations (\texttt{PatientStage}, \texttt{PatientStatus}, \texttt{TreatmentArm}, \texttt{ResponseCategory}, \texttt{AESeverity}, \texttt{ConsentStatus}, \texttt{DataLockStatus}, \texttt{PhysicalAIClassification}, \texttt{USLReadinessLevel}, and \texttt{MCPConformanceLevel}). A legal stage transition graph enforces strict linear progression through the ten stages.

\item \textbf{Orchestration layer} (10 stage modules, 5,000+ lines total): Each stage module contains a dedicated orchestrator class with stage-specific methods. For example, \texttt{stage\_01\_prescreening.py} (728 lines) implements \texttt{PreScreeningOrchestrator} with six methods: \texttt{scan\_for\_phi()}, \texttt{deidentify\_records()}, \texttt{harmonize\_clinical\_data()}, \texttt{validate\_dicom()}, \texttt{create\_patient\_record()}, and \texttt{provision\_access()}.

\item \textbf{Coordination layer} (\texttt{master\_journey.py}, 792 lines): The \texttt{MasterJourneyOrchestrator} class sequences all ten stages, maintains two critical mappings (\texttt{STAGE\_REGULATORY\_MAP} linking each stage to applicable CFR and ICH sections, and \texttt{STAGE\_MODULE\_MAP} tracking 51 activated modules across stages), and generates a comprehensive journey report upon completion.

\item \textbf{Visualization layer} (30 diagrams in \texttt{diagrams/stage\_01/} through \texttt{diagrams/stage\_10/}): Three ASCII perspective diagrams per stage---timeline and milestone tracker (\texttt{perspective\_a\_timeline.txt}), regulatory compliance tracker (\texttt{perspective\_b\_regulatory.txt}), and clinical metrics dashboard (\texttt{perspective\_c\_clinical.txt})---provide cumulative progress visualization with each stage building upon all prior stages.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regulatory Mapping}
\label{sec:regulatory-mapping}

Each of the ten stages maps to specific sections across three regulatory frameworks. The \texttt{STAGE\_REGULATORY\_MAP} in \texttt{master\_journey.py} defines these mappings explicitly. Stage 1 (Pre-Screening) addresses 7 regulatory sections including \S312.1 (scope), \S312.3 (definitions), \S50.33 (investigator notification), and ICH E6(R3) \S2.9 (records and reports). By Stage 10 (Closeout), the cumulative regulatory coverage reaches 84 sections, with the densest coverage occurring at Stage 4 (Robot Qualification, 11 new sections including all five Subpart J provisions \S312.401--405) and Stage 7 (Immunotherapy, 13 sections spanning annual IND reporting under \S312.33, protocol amendment assessment under \S312.30, and ongoing digital twin validation under ICH E6(R3) \S2.12.4).

\subsection{Explainability Through Diagrams}
\label{sec:explainability}

A defining feature of the autonomous workflow is the production of 30 text-based diagrams that serve as intermediate checkpoints for human monitoring. Each diagram is a plain-text ASCII file that can be viewed directly in a GitHub branch without rendering tools. The three perspectives were designed to address distinct stakeholder concerns:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Perspective A (Timeline):} Shows cumulative day count, stage completion status ([DONE], [ACTIVE], [PENDING]), key milestones, patient status, MCP conformance level, and modules activated. This perspective enables rapid assessment of temporal progression.

\item \textbf{Perspective B (Regulatory):} Presents a matrix of regulatory frameworks versus stages, with specific CFR and ICH section numbers addressed at each stage marked as completed or active. This perspective enables regulatory auditors to verify compliance coverage.

\item \textbf{Perspective C (Clinical):} Displays clinical metrics including tumor volume, treatment response, adverse events, digital twin validation scores, robot performance data, and recurrence risk trajectory. This perspective enables clinicians to assess patient outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

Because these diagrams are committed as plain-text files with timestamps visible in the Git history, they provide a verifiable record of the AI system's intermediate reasoning and output quality at each stage---making the autonomous workflow more explainable and credible than a single final output would be.

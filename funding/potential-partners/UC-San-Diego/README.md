# UC San Diego Clinical Trial Partnership Overview

## Purpose

This document provides a high-level overview of a potential UC San Diego pathway for advancing a verification-first AI oncology program toward institutional review, funding, and a future pancreatic cancer clinical trial.

The central objective is to determine whether UC San Diego has the clinical, surgical, regulatory, operational, and technical capacity to evaluate and potentially host the proposed research.

## Strategic Focus

UC San Diego should be approached as a direct clinical-development partner. The initial work should focus on feasibility rather than promotion.

Priority areas include:

- Pancreatic surgical oncology review.
- Gastrointestinal medical oncology review.
- Clinical-trial operations and budgeting.
- Robotic surgery and workflow feasibility.
- AI validation, cybersecurity, and human oversight.
- Regulatory planning for the drug, software, and surgical components.
- Institutional contracting, IRB review, monitoring, and data management.
- NIH, SBIR, philanthropic, and local funding opportunities.

## Recommended Sequence

1. Prepare a concise, nonconfidential scientific overview.
2. Define the exact patient population, intervention, robotic platform, and AI role.
3. Request a joint feasibility meeting with pancreatic surgery, medical oncology, and clinical-trial operations.
4. Confirm whether a qualified UC San Diego investigator is willing to lead or sponsor the study.
5. Obtain a preliminary assessment of eligible-patient volume, competing trials, operating-room capacity, pharmacy support, and expected enrollment.
6. Submit the appropriate investigator-initiated trial intake and budget materials.
7. Engage institutional regulatory, contracting, IRB, coverage-analysis, and research-administration teams.
8. Conduct a technical review of the robotic and AI workflow.
9. Clarify FDA requirements and determine whether an IND, IDE, or combination-product pathway applies.
10. Define the legal sponsor, regulatory holder, monitoring, safety, insurance, data, and intellectual-property responsibilities.
11. Secure manufacturer cooperation for investigational drug access and regulatory cross-reference.
12. Pursue federal, small-business, philanthropic, and San Diego funding in parallel.

## Required Positioning

The AI system should be described as bounded and advisory. Licensed clinicians must retain final authority over diagnosis, treatment, surgery, and safety decisions.

The proposal should clearly distinguish:

- Simulation results.
- Draft protocol concepts.
- Unvalidated software.
- Proposed clinical research.
- Established clinical evidence.

UC San Diego should not be publicly described as a partner, sponsor, trial site, or endorser unless written authorization is obtained.

## Success Criteria

A productive early outcome may include:

- An invitation to present the concept.
- Identification of a clinical principal investigator.
- Acceptance into an institutional feasibility process.
- A preliminary budget and accrual estimate.
- A technical review of the robotic and AI workflow.
- A defined regulatory and contracting pathway.
- Support for a grant or pilot application.

A patient-facing trial should proceed only after the site, manufacturer, FDA pathway, funding, sponsor responsibilities, contracts, insurance, IRB approval, and operational resources are documented.

## Working Goal

Use UC San Diego to determine whether the program is clinically credible, operationally feasible, appropriately regulated, and fundable as a staged translational research effort.

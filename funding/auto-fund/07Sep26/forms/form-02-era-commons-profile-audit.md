## What this pack is

Not a registration. The registrations were done on day 1. This is an audit of
what a reviewer would see when they open the profile, performed before a
submission rather than after one.

## The two-account rule, restated because it is the item most often wrong

If one person occupies both the Signing Official and the Program Director or
Principal Investigator roles, **two separate accounts are required**, not one
account carrying two roles. A submission made from a single dual-role account is
accepted and then rejected at validation, which costs a receipt date.

| Account | Role | Purpose |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Account A | Signing Official | Institutional authority to submit |
| Account B | Program Director / Principal Investigator | The scientific role of record |

## The audit checklist

| # | Item | What a reviewer sees if it is wrong |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Both accounts exist and are affiliated with the same institution profile | A submission that fails validation |
| 2 | ORCID [0009-0007-5457-8667](https://orcid.org/0009-0007-5457-8667) is linked to the PD/PI profile | An empty publication record on a profile whose owner has fifty-six deposits |
| 3 | The ORCID record itself lists the deposited works | The link is present but resolves to a thin record, which is worse than no link |
| 4 | Degrees, employment and contact are current | An out-of-date profile reads as an inactive applicant |
| 5 | The institution profile carries the correct DUNS successor identifier | A mismatch with SAM.gov |
| 6 | The eRA Commons username is recorded where the application will need it | An application assembled from memory has a typo in it |
| 7 | Email on the account reaches a monitored inbox | Notices about a submission go unread |
| 8 | Two-factor authentication is set up and the recovery method works | Locked out on a due date |

Item 2 is the one that matters most for this applicant specifically. The company's
whole argument rests on a public, dated, citable body of work, and a profile that
shows none of it undercuts the argument at the exact moment a reviewer forms an
impression.

## The three ways this goes wrong

1. **One account, two roles.** See above. The failure is at validation, not at
   submission, so it is discovered late.
2. **An ORCID linked but unpopulated.** The link resolves to a record with
   nothing in it, which reads worse than an absent link.
3. **A contact email that nobody reads.** eRA Commons notices are the channel by
   which a submission problem is reported, and a missed notice becomes a missed
   correction window.

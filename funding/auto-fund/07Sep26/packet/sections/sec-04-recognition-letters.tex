\section{The Three Recognition Letters}

Three presidential recognition letters have been issued to the chief executive of
ChemicalQDevice. This section fixes the wording once, so that a true statement is
made precisely rather than re-invented in each letter and drifting each time
\cite{movein2026}.

\subsection{What they are}

A fact about correspondence. Someone in the executive branch acknowledged
correspondence from this company's chief executive, three times.

\subsection{The approved wording, used verbatim wherever the subject arises}

Favorable responses to project applications have been received from federal
funders. Three presidential recognition letters have been issued to the chief
executive. Industry recipients of this company's communications have been
responsive to its initiatives. Each of those is a fact about correspondence. None
of them is an award, an agreement, a commitment, or a review of any document
produced by this company, and none is presented as one.

That paragraph already appears in the site documentation package
\cite{movein2026} and in every packet in this five-day block. It is reproduced
here so that it is copied rather than paraphrased.

\subsection{The one place they are mentioned by default}

Data room folder 07, and nowhere else. They do not appear in a funding letter, a
site letter, a foundation letter, or an investor letter unless a recipient asks
directly what federal correspondence exists, in which case the answer is the
paragraph above and nothing more.

If a recipient raises them first, the fact is confirmed in the approved wording
and the conversation moves on. No expansion, no offer to send a copy unless
asked, and no treating the topic as an opening. A recognition letter is the least
interesting true thing about this company and should occupy the least
conversational space.

\subsection{Why the discipline is worth the trouble}

A reviewer who encounters an overstated claim stops evaluating the claim and
starts evaluating the claimant. This company's entire argument rests on the
proposition that its numbers carry their own limitations and its statements can
be checked \cite{capitalplan2026}. One loosely worded sentence about a
presidential letter would cost more than the sentence could gain, and it would
cost it precisely where it matters most.

There is a simpler reason as well. The letters are real, and describing something
real inaccurately is worse than not describing it at all.

Three briefs. None of them is held, because none of them is sent to anybody: they
are internal work products that make the next four weeks faster.

## None of these is held

The four letters in [`../emails`](../emails) carry a `HOLD FOR RELEASE` line
because they reach counterparties. These three do not reach anyone until a
counterparty asks for them, so there is nothing to hold and they are finished on
the day they are written.

That distinction is the whole design of this day. A closed day cannot produce
correspondence that lands well. It can produce every internal artifact that
correspondence later depends on.

## Why brief 2 exists at all

Three presidential recognition letters have been issued to the chief executive.
That is a fact about correspondence and it is easy to describe badly. Described
loosely and often, it reads as a claim of endorsement that nobody made and that a
reviewer will test. Described once, precisely, in a data room folder with its own
disclaimer, it is simply a true statement a reader can weigh.

Brief 2 fixes the wording so that it is written once and reused rather than
re-invented in each letter, and it names the single place the letters may be
mentioned: a data room folder, and not correspondence.

## Why brief 3 is a question bank and not an answer document

A diligence process asks its own questions in its own order. An answer document
written in advance answers the questions the author expected, which are rarely
the ones asked. A question bank instead lists the twenty-two questions this
program should expect, and points each at the file that answers it, so that any
question arrives with a file already attached to it.

Four of the twenty-two currently have **no** answer, and brief 3 says so in its
own table rather than hiding them among the eighteen that do. Those four are the
ones worth working on.

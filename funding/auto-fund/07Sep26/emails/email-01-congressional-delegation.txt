Dear Health Legislative Aide,

I am a San Diego constituent and the chief executive of a small company here, and
I am writing about one narrow federal question rather than about a bill.

ChemicalQDevice develops verification-first artificial intelligence for oncology
clinical trials. Its current program is a Phase 1 study in resectable pancreatic
ductal adenocarcinoma combining a perioperative RAS(ON) inhibitor, a robotic
pancreaticoduodenectomy, and an advisory-only on-premises language model whose
output never reaches an actuator. On August 26, 2026 the FDA approved Rasonque
(daraxonrasib) in the metastatic setting; the perioperative use in resectable
disease that this program proposes remains investigational.

The narrow question. A system of this shape is a drug, a device, and a software
component at once, and there is no published clock on how long a combined review
of that kind takes. A small business can plan against a long clock. It cannot
plan against an unknown one, and the difference determines whether a company like
this one can raise capital against a regulatory timeline at all.

I am not asking for support for any application, for an appropriation, or for
intervention with any agency. I am asking whether the office has an interest in
the general question of published review timelines for combined artificial
intelligence and robotic clinical workflows, and, if so, whether a short
informational meeting with the district office would be useful.

One boundary I want to state before anyone asks. No federal award funds are used
for any part of this communication, because no federal award exists; and were one
to exist, lobbying of any kind would be paid from company or private funds and
never from award funds. The same activity is lawful with one source of funds and
unlawful with another, and this company keeps the two separate as a matter of
structure rather than of intention.

Three facts about the company, offered so that the office can judge whether this
is worth a half hour. Its entire technical and regulatory output is public in one
repository, including a Phase 1 protocol, an investigational new drug
application, and quantitative trial simulations with their verification
notebooks. Favorable responses to project applications have been received from
federal funders, and three presidential recognition letters have been issued to
me; each of those is a fact about correspondence and none of them is an award, an
agreement, or a review. And the company is one person with a defined staffing
plan, not a claim of scale it does not have.

I would welcome a short meeting at the district office, and I am equally content
to be told this is not the right question for this office.

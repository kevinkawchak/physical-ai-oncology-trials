Two briefs, both looking forward rather than back. One says what the next thirty
days hold. The other says what every week holds from here, so that the next
five-day block is a substitution of content into a known frame rather than a
fresh design.

## Why exactly two

The other four days each needed a technical brief for a specific recipient. This
day has no new recipient: its letters are follow-ups, a confirmation, and an
execution instruction, and every one of them refers to a brief written earlier in
the block. What this day needs instead is the two documents that make the block
repeatable, and a third brief would be a summary of the four days rather than a
tool for the next four.

The five-day record itself lives in the packet as its closing table rather than
as a brief, because it is the one page a person who missed the week reads, and it
belongs in the document that person is given.

## The rule brief 1 obeys

**Every row waits on somebody other than this company, or it is not in the
pipeline.** An item whose next action belongs to this company is not pipeline; it
is work, and it goes on a to-do list rather than into a tracker. Confusing the
two produces a pipeline that looks busy and moves nothing.

## The rule brief 2 obeys

The cadence fits on one page and names a time budget for each weekday. A cadence
that does not fit on a page will not be followed, and a cadence without a time
budget expands until it consumes the work it was meant to support.

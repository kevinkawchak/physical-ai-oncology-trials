# The Thirty-Day Pipeline

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
Internal. Every row waits on somebody other than this company, or it is not in
this table.

---

## The rule this table obeys

An item whose next action belongs to this company is not pipeline. It is work,
and it belongs on a to-do list. Confusing the two produces a pipeline that looks
busy and moves nothing, and a founder who reads it feels productive without being
so.

Every row below therefore names an external party, what they hold, and what this
company does when the interval elapses without a reply.

## The four items that cannot be chased at all

| Item | Why not |
|:--|:--|
| The FDA classification | It arrives when it arrives, and an applicant who chases a classification request is an applicant the office remembers |
| A foundation cycle | Published dates are not negotiable |
| A developer submission window | Opened by the developer's own calendar |
| An institutional review board calendar | Meets when it meets |

Naming these separately matters. A founder with time and anxiety will chase
something, and it should not be one of these four.

## What the thirty days actually produce, if nothing goes wrong

One FDA classification, one sponsor-investigator determination, one instrument
selection with a written ownership answer, one site routing outcome, and between
zero and three federal replies. That is five decisions, four of which unblock
something else, and it is a reasonable month for a company of one person.

If nothing arrives at all, the four unanswered diligence questions are unchanged
and the stop conditions are unchanged, which is the honest downside and is
already written down.

Five letters. One is a filed record, three are follow-ups sent only where the
three-business-day interval has elapsed, and one transmits the order queue with
this morning's limit written into it.

## Letter 1 is filed rather than sent

[`email-01-release-cover-note.txt`](email-01-release-cover-note.txt) is addressed
to the chief executive and is filed in the data room rather than sent to anyone.
A release with no record of what left and when is a release nobody can
reconstruct three weeks later, when a recipient replies to a letter and the reply
has to be matched to what was actually asked.

It is written as a letter rather than as a note because the form forces the
information to be complete: a `TO`, a time, an itemized list, and a signature.

## The three-business-day rule, applied

No follow-up goes out inside three business days of the letter it follows. The
rule comes from the escalation guidance in the partner research, and it is
applied to federal offices as well as to institutions, because a program officer
who receives a follow-up on day two reads an applicant who is counting hours.

| Letter it follows | Business days elapsed | Follow-up sent today |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Day 1 federal letters | 3 | Yes, letter 2 |
| Day 2 developer letter | 3 | Yes, letter 3, on the window question only |
| Day 3 site letters | 2 | **No** |
| Day 3 foundation letters | 2 | **No** |
| Day 4 held letters | 0, released today | No, they are being sent for the first time |

The holiday does not count as a business day, which is what makes today the third
business day after day 1 and only the second after day 3.

Letter 4 is conditional: it is sent only if an institution replied, and it is
left unsent if none has. A confirmation letter with nothing to confirm is a
second request wearing a confirmation's clothes, and recipients notice.

## What a follow-up may and may not contain

| May | May not |
|:--|:--|
| One new fact the first letter could not carry | A restatement of the first letter |
| A shorter version of the original question | A new question the first letter did not ask |
| An offer to answer anything unclear | A deadline, implied or stated |
| The original letter quoted at the foot for reference | Any suggestion that a reply is overdue |

A follow-up that repeats the first letter tells the recipient the first letter is
not worth finding. A follow-up carrying one new fact gives them a reason to open
the thread.

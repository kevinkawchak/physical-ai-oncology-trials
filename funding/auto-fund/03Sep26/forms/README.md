Two packs. Both are complete. **Neither is filed on this day, and filing either
one early would be a mistake that cannot be taken back.**

## Why neither is filed on this day

A Form D filing is a public record that an offering exists. It is due within
fifteen days **after** the first sale of securities, not before, and there is no
first sale, because there is no offering and no instrument has been selected
\[[SEC Rule 506(b)](https://www.sec.gov/education/smallbusiness/exemptofferings/rule506b)\].

Filing before a first sale publishes an offering into EDGAR that has not
happened. Every subsequent conversation then begins with an investor asking about
a filing that describes terms the company has not agreed, and there is no clean
way to withdraw the impression.

The California Section 25102(f) notice follows the same logic and the same
trigger.

So on this day the packs are **read**, the fields are checked against the
instrument comparison, and the gaps that only counsel can close are marked. That
is the whole of the work.

## What each pack marks as counsel-only

Both packs mark, in their own field tables, the fields that must not be completed
without counsel: the exemption claimed, the date of first sale, the total offering
amount, and the investor count by accreditation status. Those four determine
whether the exemption holds, and a wrong answer in any of them is not a
typographical error.

## The prerequisite that gates both

An EDGAR filer identifier. Obtaining one takes days, not minutes, and it cannot
be obtained inside the fifteen-day window after a first sale without pressure.
Pack 1 records how to obtain it and says plainly that this is the one item worth
doing before an offering exists.

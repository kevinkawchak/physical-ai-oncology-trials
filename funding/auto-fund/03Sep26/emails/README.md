Five letters in plain text. Letter 1 opens a commercial relationship. Letters 2
and 3 ask for introductions. Letter 4 engages counsel. Letter 5 prepares the
account that would receive a subscription.

## The line none of these letters crosses

None of the five describes an offering, states a term as available, or invites a
subscription. Under Rule 506(b) an issuer may not generally solicit at all, and a
letter that reads as an offer to a person the issuer has no prior relationship
with is the exact thing the exemption does not permit
\[[SEC Rule 506(b)](https://www.sec.gov/education/smallbusiness/exemptofferings/rule506b)\].

So letters 2 and 3 ask for a screening conversation and a read of the public
repository. They describe the company, the program, the evidence, and the shape
of the capital question. They do not describe a security. If a conversation
follows and a relationship is established, an offering is a separate step taken
with counsel, which is what letter 4 is for.

## Addresses that are bracketed, and why

Every address this repository already records is filled in. Addresses this
repository does not record are left in brackets with the exact page to obtain
them from, because inventing an address is worse than a bracket: a wrong address
either bounces or, worse, reaches a stranger.

## What letter 1 asks for, in the developer's own vocabulary

Carried from `../../../potential-partners/UC-San-Diego/priority-steps.md` §2 and
unchanged except where the approval makes a request obsolete:

1. A scientific feasibility meeting.
2. Whether the company would consider drug supply, research funding, or both.
3. Access to the current Investigator's Brochure, or to the labeled product
   information now that the agent is approved.
4. A Letter of Authorization allowing FDA to reference chemistry, manufacturing,
   pharmacology, toxicology and safety information in the company's files.
5. Medical monitor and pharmacovigilance requirements.
6. Guidance on dose, washout, perioperative interruption, drug accountability and
   safety reporting.
7. Assessment of overlap with company-sponsored studies.
8. The date of the next External Sponsored Research submission window.

The attachment limit is carried unchanged as well: a one-page nonconfidential
overview, a two-page clinical synopsis, a biosketch, a preliminary budget range,
and a one-page dependency matrix. **The full LaTeX package is not sent.**

## The four positioning corrections in every `BEFORE SENDING` block

1. Daraxonrasib is not described as first in human.
2. The robotic configuration is specified at the site agreement and not before.
3. No institution is described as a partner, sponsor, site, or endorser.
4. The approval covers the metastatic setting and is nowhere described as
   covering the perioperative use this program proposes.

\section{The Firewall the Instrument Must Leave Intact}

The chief executive is chief executive of the sponsor and holds the whole of it.
He is not a clinical investigator on the trial this program describes, will not
be one, and is nowhere described as one. The investigator role sits wholly with
the clinical site.

That is not modesty. It is the only arrangement under which the financial
interest a founder necessarily has in his own company does not become an
investigator financial interest that a reviewer must weigh against the data
\cite{ecfr2026part54,fdafindisclosure2013}.

Because all four would fire at once, the separation cannot be a matter of degree.
A Phase 1 3+3 escalation carries no randomization, so the firewall binds on dose
assignment, dose-limiting toxicity adjudication, and the analysis plan instead.
That wording is carried forward deliberately, so that it still binds when a Phase
2 successor introduces randomization \cite{phase2proto}.

\subsection{What each instrument does to the firewall}

A simple agreement for future equity and a convertible note leave it untouched
until conversion: holders take no board seat and no protective provisions at
signing. A priced preferred round usually brings both, which introduces persons
with governance rights and an economic interest in the study outcome. That is not
itself an investigator interest, but it changes who is in the room when dose and
analysis questions are discussed, and the value of a firewall is that it is easy
to describe.

Each of the four is a guard rather than a step. The ownership answer gates the
instrument because the answer determines the instrument and not the reverse. The
filings are prepared and not filed because a Form D filed before a first sale
publishes an offering that does not exist \cite{secformd2026}. The prior
relationship is a condition about the past rather than the present, which is what
makes the no-general-solicitation rule hard to satisfy retroactively. And the
fifteen-day clock starts at the sale and is not extended.

\subsection{The four questions this section hands to counsel}

Who holds the investigational new drug application, the sponsor or the developer.
Whether a site physician could serve as sponsor-investigator, and whether that
would be better or worse for the firewall than the current split. Who performs
safety reporting, monitoring, pharmacovigilance, drug accountability and annual
reports. And who pays for insurance, indemnification, subject injury, and device
and software validation \cite{ecfr2026part312records}.

The applicant, the site principal investigator, the FDA sponsor, the application
holder and the device manufacturer do not have to be the same entity. Their
responsibilities do have to be explicit, and they are not yet.

\subsection{Why this is written down before an instrument is chosen}

A conflict boundary described after a financing reads as a defense. The same
boundary described before one reads as a design, and a reviewer can tell the
difference. The four triggers in Table 9 would fire whatever instrument is
chosen, so the question a reviewer will actually ask is not whether the founder
has a financial interest, which is obvious, but whether anyone with such an
interest can influence a dose decision, a toxicity adjudication, or an analysis.

The answer is architectural rather than procedural: those three decisions sit
with the site, the analysis plan is pre-specified before the first participant,
and the model process holds no write credential to the data capture system
\cite{onpremwhippl}. None of that changes if a holder is added to the cap table,
and that invariance is the property this section exists to establish.

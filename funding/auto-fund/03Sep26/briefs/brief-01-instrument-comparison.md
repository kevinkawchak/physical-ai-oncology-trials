## The three candidates, scored on eight attributes

| Attribute | Post-money SAFE | Convertible note | Priced preferred seed |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Time to first close | Days | Days to weeks | Weeks to months |
| Legal cost to issuer | Lowest | Low | Highest |
| Does it accrue interest | No | Yes | No |
| Does it have a maturity date | No | Yes, and it can force a decision | No |
| Ownership transferred at signing | None until conversion | None until conversion | Immediately |
| Cap table clarity for a later round | Good if one cap, poor if stacked | Same | Best |
| Governance given away | None at signing | None at signing | Board seat and protective provisions are usual |
| **Effect on the SBIR ownership answer** | **None until conversion** | **None until conversion** | **Can change it, if majority ownership transfers** |

The last row is the one that decides this for a company in this position, and it
is the reason the comparison exists at all.

## Why the last row dominates

Under 13 CFR 121.702, majority ownership by multiple venture capital operating
companies, hedge funds, or private equity firms changes SBIR eligibility and at
some agencies requires a separate authorization. The SBA Company Registry asks
that question directly, and the answer is re-checked at every financing event
rather than annually.

An instrument that has not converted transfers no ownership, so the registry
answer on the day after signing is the same as the day before. A priced round
that transfers majority ownership can change it.

That does not make a priced round wrong. It makes it a decision that must be
taken with the federal route's status in front of the decision-maker, rather than
discovered afterward at a submission deadline. **This reading is stated as the
company's own and is sent to counsel for confirmation or correction before any
instrument is signed**, which is item two of the engagement letter in
[`../emails/email-04-securities-counsel-engagement.txt`](../emails/email-04-securities-counsel-engagement.txt).

## The tranche shape, if the instrument is convertible

Described as a plan under consideration, not offered.

| Tranche | Purpose | Trigger to open it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| First | Site feasibility, regulatory pre-submission, verification harness | Instrument selected and counsel engaged |
| Second | Site agreement, IRB submission, first-participant readiness | A written site feasibility outcome |
| Third | Trial conduct beyond what the federal route funds | An accepted investigational new drug application |

Tranching against evidence rather than against a calendar is deliberate. A holder
who funds a tranche is funding a stated next artifact, and a tranche that does
not open because its trigger did not fire is a smaller loss than a full raise
spent against a milestone that never arrived.

## What a holder is buying, and what they are not

Buying: a position in a company whose regulatory and computational output is
already public, dated, and citable, in a disease with the widest gap between
incidence and survival in the United States, working on the workflow question
that remains after the agent question resolved externally in August 2026.

Not buying: any interest in the approved drug, any interest in the developer, any
clinical result, or any agreement with any institution. No drug supply agreement,
letter of authorization, or regulatory cross-reference exists. No agreement of any
kind exists with any clinical site.

## The recommendation this brief makes

Take the instrument that leaves the SBIR ownership answer unchanged until a
deliberate decision is made to change it, and take it with one valuation cap
rather than several. A stack of instruments at different caps produces a cap
table that a later institutional round has to unwind before it can price, and
unwinding it costs more than the difference between the caps.

That is a recommendation to the chief executive about the company's own capital
structure. It is not advice to any other person and it is not an offer.

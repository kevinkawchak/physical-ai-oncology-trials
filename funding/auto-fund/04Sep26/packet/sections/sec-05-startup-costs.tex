\section{Site Start-Up Money, Allocated and Committed to Nobody}

\$420,000 of private capital is allocated to site start-up. \textbf{Nothing has been
committed.} No agreement of any kind exists with any institution, and a company
that commits start-up money to an institution that has not agreed to anything has
converted capital into enthusiasm.

Each trigger is an artifact somebody else produces. That is deliberate. A stage
that releases on a date releases whether or not the work it funds is possible; a
stage that releases on an artifact cannot.

\subsection{Why this pool is held short and not laddered}

The operating reserve is laddered because its draws are known in advance
\cite{treasuryauctions2026}. This pool's draws are not: their timing depends on
when a site names a principal investigator, when a budget questionnaire comes
back, and when an institutional review board meets. A ladder against unknown
draws guarantees that a maturity eventually has to be broken to pay an invoice,
which is the loss a ladder exists to prevent. So the pool sits at seventy percent
in a government sweep, twenty percent in one short-duration Treasury fund entered
on a limit order, and ten percent in a single three-month bill. The yield given
up is the price of not being forced.

\subsection{How this reconciles to the program budget}

The five-year program is \$700,000 per year and \$3,500,000 in direct cost, and
site personnel sit inside the \$521,000 personnel line of that frame
\cite{tenapps2026,movein2026}. This pool is one time, is private capital rather
than federal award funds, and pays for start-up work that precedes an award.

That last point is the one a program officer would check. Start-up work performed
before an award start date is not chargeable to that award, which is precisely
why it is funded privately here, and why federal award funds and private capital
are never commingled in either direction.

\subsection{What a site is told about this in a first meeting}

That the money exists, that it is segregated, that it is committed to nobody, and
that the first stage releases on a written response rather than on a signature.
A site that hears a number in a first meeting hears seriousness; a site that
hears a number and then discovers the number was aspirational hears something
else, which is why the ceilings in Table 14 are ceilings rather than forecasts.

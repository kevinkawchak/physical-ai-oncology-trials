One instruction set. Days 1 and 2 dealt with the company's own operating reserve.
This one deals with a different pool: the $420,000 site start-up line, which
exists to be spent on a schedule this day's letters are trying to set.

## Why a site start-up line needs its own instruction

The operating reserve funds the company. The site start-up line funds an
institution's start-up work: coverage analysis, contracting, institutional review
board submission, and the first months of coordinator and regulatory time. Those
are invoiced by a third party on a schedule the company does not control, in
amounts it will not know until a budget questionnaire comes back.

A pool with an unknown draw schedule cannot be laddered. Laddering it would
guarantee that at some point a maturity has to be broken to pay an invoice, which
is exactly what a ladder is built to avoid. So this pool is held short and liquid
by design, and the instruction says so rather than defaulting to the same shape
as the operating reserve.

## The rule that governs the whole pool

**No dollar of the site start-up line is committed before a written site
feasibility outcome exists.** A company that commits start-up money to an
institution that has not agreed to anything has converted capital into
enthusiasm. The commitment trigger is a written outcome from a named institution,
and it is the same trigger that opens the second tranche of the private capital
in day 2.

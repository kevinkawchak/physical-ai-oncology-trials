Dear Digital Trials Center,

This is an informational notice rather than a request, and I have written it so that it can be ignored without cost if the work is not of interest.

ChemicalQDevice is a San Diego company publishing a verification-first artificial intelligence portfolio for oncology clinical trials. The portfolio is public in full at https://github.com/kevinkawchak/physical-ai-oncology-trials and I am writing to invite an independent evaluation of its verification, provenance, simulation and regulatory-document methods by anyone at Scripps for whom that is interesting.

Five items are the ones worth an hour.

1. QSP Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer AI Clinical Trial Simulation, from protocol to prediction, code, verification and validation, and playbook. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17001137
2. A 100,000-patient, 24-month, five-arm pancreatic cancer in silico Phase III simulation, in triplicate. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15735068
3. Phase 1 Pancreatic Cancer Trial: Efficient LLM Document Guidance. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21018646
4. Investigational New Drug Application for a Phase 1 LLM-advised robotic Whipple trial. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21097442
5. Oncology Trial Principal Investigator LLM Adoption Guide, with its verification, validation and uncertainty quantification materials. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20843290

For each of the five, the repository carries the public link, the compiled PDF, a summary, the methods used, the human review process, the known limitations, the regulatory status, and a statement that the work is investigational and has not established clinical safety or efficacy. Three of the quantitative results are in silico or digital twin results and are labeled as such wherever they appear.

How the artificial intelligence work is actually done, since that is the part a digital trials group would want to interrogate. Multiple frontier systems are used for independent drafting, review, and discrepancy identification: one for repository-scale production, one for independent verification and document analysis, and one for a second review pass. The human investigator retains responsibility for every scientific, clinical and regulatory conclusion. Nothing is accepted because a model produced it.

There is a clinical program behind the portfolio and I mention it once, at the end, deliberately. It is a Phase 1 study of perioperative daraxonrasib with a robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy and an advisory-only model, in KRAS-mutated pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. On August 26, 2026 the FDA approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) in the metastatic setting; the perioperative use in resectable disease that this program proposes is not the approved setting and remains investigational. I am not asking Scripps to host anything in this letter.

Two disclosures. I am approaching UC San Diego in parallel on the same day, by a different route and with a different question; neither institution is being asked for exclusivity and neither is described to the other as preferred. And no agreement of any kind exists with any institution.

Dear Research Grants Team,

One question, asked before an application rather than inside one.

ChemicalQDevice is a San Diego company preparing an investigator-initiated Phase
1 study in resectable and borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated pancreatic
ductal adenocarcinoma: perioperative daraxonrasib with a staged robotic
pancreaticoduodenectomy and an advisory-only on-premises large language model
whose output never reaches an actuator. The FDA approved Rasonque
(daraxonrasib) in the metastatic setting on August 26, 2026. The perioperative
use in resectable disease that this study proposes is not the approved setting,
remains investigational, and still requires an investigational new drug
application.

The question. Is a Phase 1 clinical study whose sponsor is a for-profit small
business, with an academic cancer center as the clinical site under a separate
agreement, eligible under any current grants program mechanism? If it is not,
saying so plainly is more useful to me than an encouragement to apply.

If it is eligible, three follow-on questions. Which mechanism. When the current
cycle opens and closes. And whether the mechanism requires a named clinical site
at the letter-of-intent stage or only at full application.

Why this study and not another. In June 2025 this company's method identified a
daraxonrasib combination as its lead candidate from a review of forty pancreatic
ductal adenocarcinoma meta-analyses. In August 2025 a ten-arm quantitative
systems pharmacology simulation returned median overall survival of 12.8 months
against 5.4 for chemotherapy. In May 2026 RASolute 302 reported 13.2 against 6.6
in previously treated metastatic disease, and in August 2026 the agent was
approved. The proximity between the 2025 simulation and the 2026 readout is a
chronology observation and a hypothesis-supporting one, not a validation claim,
and three differences are material: 1000 simulated against 241 enrolled, a
combination against a single agent, and KRAS G12C against a primarily G12D and
G12V population.

What has not happened, stated in the same paragraph. No agreement of any kind
exists with any clinical site. No drug supply agreement, letter of authorization,
or regulatory cross-reference is in place with the agent's developer. No
institutional review board has reviewed anything. No patient has been treated.

Everything the company has produced is public at
https://github.com/kevinkawchak/physical-ai-oncology-trials,
including the Phase 1 protocol, the investigational new drug application, and the
simulation code with its verification notebooks. A reviewer can form a view
without a call.

I would welcome a short reply, including a no.

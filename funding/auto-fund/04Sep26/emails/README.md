Five letters. Two open institutional routes that run at the same time, one asks
the question that gates both, and two ask foundations a question about their own
mechanisms rather than for money.

## The disclosure rule between the two routes

Letters 1 and 2 go to two institutions in the same city on the same day. Each
letter says so. That is not a courtesy flourish; it is the only version of this
that survives contact with reality, because both institutions run clinical trials
in a small community and will learn of each other's involvement in any case. An
institution that hears it first from the letter reads a company that is
straightforward. An institution that hears it from a colleague reads a company
that was not.

Neither letter asks either institution for exclusivity, and neither describes the
other as preferred.

## What no letter on this day does

No letter asks any institution to endorse a grant application. Senior leadership
is not asked to endorse anything before a surgeon, an oncologist and a trials
office have reviewed basic feasibility, which is the sequence the partner
research sets out and the sequence that respects the people in it.

No letter sends engineering detail. A confidentiality agreement is put in place
before confidential engineering detail or unpublished manufacturer information is
shared, and the route for that agreement is a question in letter 5 rather than an
assumption in letter 1.

## The four positioning corrections in every `BEFORE SENDING` block

1. Daraxonrasib is not described as first in human.
2. The robotic configuration is specified at the site agreement and not before.
   No manufacturer, model, arm count, or software version is asserted.
3. No institution is described as a partner, sponsor, site, or endorser, and no
   agreement of any kind exists.
4. The approval covers the metastatic setting and is nowhere described as
   covering the perioperative use this program proposes.

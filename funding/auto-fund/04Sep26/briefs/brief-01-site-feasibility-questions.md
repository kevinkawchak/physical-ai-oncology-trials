# The Five Questions a First Site Meeting Has to Settle

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
Prepared as the agenda for a 45-minute feasibility meeting. Independent work, not
medical or regulatory advice, and not endorsed by the FDA, NIH, HHS, an IRB, ICH,
or any sponsor.

---

## How to use this

Every question below is answerable in the meeting by people who are already in
the room. None requires a document to be read in advance, a committee to convene,
or a decision to be escalated. If a question cannot be answered in the room, the
right outcome is to say so and name who can, which is itself a useful answer.

## The five questions

### 1. Is the patient population and the perioperative concept clinically supportable here?

| A yes unlocks | A no costs |
|:--|:--|
| Everything else on this list | Nothing else on this list matters, and the right response is to stop rather than to negotiate |

The study is a Phase 1, open-label, single-arm, 3+3 dose escalation in up to 18
participants with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated pancreatic
ductal adenocarcinoma, combining perioperative daraxonrasib with a staged robotic
pancreaticoduodenectomy. Rasonque (daraxonrasib) was approved in the metastatic
setting on August 26, 2026; this use is not that setting and remains
investigational.

### 2. Could a qualified pancreatic surgeon here serve as site principal investigator, and a gastrointestinal medical oncologist lead systemic therapy and safety?

| A yes unlocks | A no costs |
|:--|:--|
| The regulatory structure, because the investigator role must sit with the site | The sponsor cannot supply this role: the chief executive holds the whole of the sponsor and every 21 CFR 54.2 trigger would fire if he held both roles |

### 3. What is the annual eligible-patient count, and what competes for it?

| A yes unlocks | A no costs |
|:--|:--|
| An enrollment period a funder can believe | An 18-participant escalation with three competing protocols on the same population is a five-year enrollment, and a five-year enrollment is a different trial |

Also needed: operating room capacity for a research case, pharmacy support for an
investigational perioperative agent, and pathology and specimen processing
turnaround.

### 4. Is the robotic platform actually available, and is the proposed workflow permissible on it?

| A yes unlocks | A no costs |
|:--|:--|
| A configuration that can be named in a regulatory submission | The submission cannot be written: no site can assess feasibility or regulatory status without an identifiable platform |

**No manufacturer, model, arm count, or software version is asserted by this
company.** The configuration is specified at the site agreement and not before,
and this question is where it gets specified. What is needed from the site: which
systems are available, which instruments, which software versions, what
networking restrictions apply, what cybersecurity requirements apply, and whether
a recorded research session is permissible.

### 5. Would the institution host an investigator-initiated trial sponsored by a small San Diego company?

| A yes unlocks | A no costs |
|:--|:--|
| The contracting, budgeting and review workstreams | Everything above is academic. This question is asked last because it is the one most likely to be answered by policy rather than by the people in the room |

## The one item that gates all five

Which party is capable of serving as **sponsor-investigator**. The applicant, the
site principal investigator, the FDA sponsor, the investigational new drug
application holder and the device manufacturer do not have to be the same entity,
but their responsibilities have to be explicit, and today they are consistent
only with this company's assumption about them.

That determination is requested separately from clinical trial support services,
because it is a regulatory question rather than a clinical one and it should not
consume a clinical meeting.

## What this company is not asking for in a first meeting

Not an endorsement. Not a hosting commitment. Not a letter of support for a grant
application. Not a decision from senior leadership before a surgeon, an
oncologist and a trials office have formed a view. And not the sharing of any
confidential engineering detail before a confidentiality agreement is in place.

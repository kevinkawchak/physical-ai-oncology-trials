Two briefs, not three. This day's technical content is a question list and a
positioning statement; a third brief would restate one of them, and a document
that restates itself is a document a busy reader stops trusting.

## Who these are for

Brief 1 goes to a site principal investigator, a clinical trials office, and a
budget analyst. It is the agenda for a 45-minute meeting, written so that the
meeting can happen without anyone preparing for it.

Brief 2 goes to either institution and to the chief executive. It explains why
two institutions are being approached at once, what each is being asked, and what
neither is being asked.

## The rule brief 1 obeys

Every question in brief 1 is answerable in a 45-minute meeting by people who are
already in the room. No question requires a document to be read in advance, a
committee to convene, or a decision to be escalated. A feasibility meeting whose
agenda requires preparation is a feasibility meeting that gets rescheduled.

## The rule brief 2 obeys

Neither institution is described as preferred, and neither is described as a
fallback. Both letters disclose the other approach. A company that ranks two
institutions in writing has created a document that will eventually be read by
the one it ranked second.

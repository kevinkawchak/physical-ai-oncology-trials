# Why Two Institutions Are Approached on the Same Day

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
Written to be read by either institution. Independent work, not medical or
regulatory advice.

---

## The short version

Two La Jolla institutions are approached on the same day, by two different
routes, asking two different questions. Each letter discloses the other. Neither
institution is asked for exclusivity, and neither is described to the other as
preferred or as a fallback.

## Why parallel and not sequential

A sequential approach costs a quarter. A first institution takes four to eight
weeks to route an unsolicited feasibility request to the right surgeon; if the
answer is no, the second institution starts from zero after that. For an
eighteen-participant Phase 1 with a complete protocol already deposited, spending
a quarter to learn a routing answer is the expensive option.

A parallel approach costs something too, and it is worth naming: two institutions
in one small clinical community will learn of each other's involvement. The only
question is whether they learn it from the letter or from a colleague. Learning
it from the letter is strictly better for everyone, which is why both letters
carry the disclosure.

## What each is asked, and why the asks differ

| | UC San Diego, Moores Cancer Center | Scripps Research and Scripps Health |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Route | Escalation to leadership on a published path, after an August 2026 feasibility request | An inbound visibility notice to the Digital Trials Center |
| The ask | A routing decision: who is the right site principal investigator and trial operations contact | A read of the public portfolio, and a view on whether the method is of interest |
| What leads | The clinical program | The artificial intelligence work |
| What follows | The artificial intelligence work | The clinical program, mentioned once at the end |
| Why | An August request already exists and should be escalated, not repeated | No prior request exists, and the Digital Trials Center's own mandate is method collaboration rather than hosting |

The asks differ because the institutions' entry points differ. Approaching a
digital trials group with a request to host an experimental surgical operation
would be asking the wrong department for the wrong thing.

## What neither is asked

Neither is asked to endorse a grant application. Neither is asked to commit to
hosting. Neither is asked to agree to anything. And neither is sent confidential
engineering detail before a confidentiality agreement is in place.

Senior leadership at either institution is not asked to form a view before a
surgeon, a medical oncologist and a trials office have reviewed basic
feasibility. That sequence is not deference for its own sake: an endorsement
obtained above the people who have to do the work is an endorsement that
evaporates at the first operational question.

## The clinical introductions that are deliberately deferred

Two Scripps clinicians are relevant to this program by specialty. No public
professional email address was located for either, and this company will not
guess one. If Scripps responds, the introduction is requested through Scripps
Clinical Research Services or the chief research officer rather than attempted
directly. That rule holds for any clinician at any institution: an introduction
requested through the institution is a introduction the institution knows about.

## The escalation rule, applied to both

If there is no response within three business days, the original request is
forwarded once with a short note, to the contact one level up on the published
path. It is forwarded once and not repeatedly. A second escalation inside a week
converts a routing question into an impression of the company, and the impression
outlasts the question.

## What this brief does not claim

No agreement of any kind exists with UC San Diego, with Moores Cancer Center,
with Scripps Research, with Scripps Health, or with any other institution.
Neither institution has reviewed, endorsed, or agreed to host anything. Both are
named at the feasibility stage only. No institutional review board has reviewed
anything in this program.

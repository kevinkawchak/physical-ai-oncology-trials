Dear NIH Director's Pioneer Award Program Staff,

I wrote to this office on July 10, 2026 as an independent scientist, asking whether the Director's Pioneer Award was the right mechanism for a Phase 1 pancreatic cancer study combining a perioperative RAS(ON) inhibitor, a staged robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy, and an on-premises advisory model. Two questions in that letter concerned scope and the availability of a pre-application conversation. This letter adds two narrower questions, and states one fact that has changed since.

On August 26, 2026 the FDA approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) as the first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer. My proposed use is perioperative, in resectable and borderline-resectable disease, and remains investigational. The approval does not make the proposed use approved, and this letter does not describe it as approved.

What the approval changes for a Pioneer Award reviewer is narrower than it may sound and I would rather state it modestly. The July letter asked a reviewer to accept three coupled uncertainties at once. One of them has since resolved on the public record. What remains is an integrated surgical and advisory workflow whose novelty claim is unchanged, and whose evidentiary base now includes an external checkpoint that the reviewer can date rather than take on the applicant's word.

I also want to record, once and without overstatement, a chronology that bears on the individual-investigator case this mechanism exists to fund. In June 2025 this company's method identified a daraxonrasib combination as its lead candidate from a review of forty pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma meta-analyses. In August 2025 a ten-arm quantitative systems pharmacology simulation returned 12.8 months median overall survival against 5.4 for chemotherapy. In May 2026 RASolute 302 reported 13.2 against 6.6 in previously treated metastatic disease, a 2.0-fold ratio against the simulated 2.4-fold. In August 2026 the agent was approved. Each of those is a dated public artifact produced by one person working from one public repository without institutional support or indirect cost. The proximity between the 2025 simulation and the 2026 readout is a chronology observation and a hypothesis-supporting one; it is not a validation claim, and the three material differences are 1000 simulated against 241 enrolled, a combination against a single agent, and KRAS G12C against a primarily G12D and G12V population.

Two questions, both narrow.

First, are the tentative science codes 2 CTR and 4 IE still the right pair for a combined drug, device, and software workflow whose agent is now approved in a different setting, or would 8 BCB be the better second code?

Second, does the 51 percent research effort expectation in years one through three apply exactly as written to a chief executive of the applicant small business, or is there an established accommodation where the applicant is the firm rather than an institution?

I would welcome a short call at your convenience, and I remain grateful for the program's time.

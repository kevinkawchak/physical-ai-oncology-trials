Three briefs in markdown, lightly formatted, tables where a table is the right
shape, and no LaTeX furniture anywhere. Each is short enough to be pasted into
the body of a message when a recipient asks not to open an attachment.

## Who these are for

A program officer's technical staff, a foundation's scientific reviewer, and an
investor's analyst all share one habit: they read plain text first and open a PDF
second. The packet in [`../packet`](../packet) is for the second reader. These
three are for the first.

The division is deliberate and it is the reason instruction B of the master
prompt separates the two formats. A technical reader given a fourteen-page
typeset document as a first contact will skim it. The same reader given eight
hundred words of markdown will read all of it.

## The rule every brief obeys

Every quantitative claim appears in the same sentence or the same table row as
the limitation its own authors stated. A brief that separates a result from its
caveat by so much as a paragraph invites the caveat to be dropped when the brief
is quoted, and a caveat dropped by a third party is still the author's problem.

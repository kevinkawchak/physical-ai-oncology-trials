\section{Set B: The Medical Oncology Perspective}

\subsection{What Leads in Set B}

In the five medical oncology applications the drug and the patient selection
lead. Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) is a RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor given
perioperatively in KRAS G12-mutated resectable or borderline-resectable disease,
and the operation is described in full but as the instrumented setting that
makes the pharmacology measurable.

The endpoint table reorders accordingly: dose-limiting toxicity and the 3+3
escalation first, then sampling timed around the resection, then pathologic
response, ctDNA clearance, and 24-month overall survival. The operation matters
for one reason no metastatic study can supply: it is the only setting yielding
paired pre-treatment and on-treatment human tumour tissue under controlled
timing.

\subsection{Applications 06 to 10}

\textbf{06, Foundation for the NIH.} A pre-competitive question no party owns:
what happens pharmacologically when a RAS(ON) inhibitor is given around a
curative-intent resection. The one shared output is owned by none of the three
contributors, which is what makes it pre-competitive.

\textbf{07, HHMI Investigator Program.} Tests the one place the report's
person-based argument might not reach, an investigator with no academic
appointment. The programme is written as a state machine rather than a set of
aims, and every failure transition re-enters it.

\textbf{08, NCI Cancer Therapy Evaluation Program.} The most clinically
technical, because a concept review is a technical review. An adverse event here
may be attributable to the drug, the operation, or the advisory system, and the
dose decision depends on which, so attribution is adjudicated blind before the
toxicity window closes.

\textbf{09, Convergent Research.} The only application whose organization states
its own end date: no year six is budgeted, only one of three releases depends on
the trial succeeding, and every artifact has a recipient class fixed in year one.

\textbf{10, UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center.} Different in kind: addressed to
the partner institution, asking for a 45-minute feasibility meeting rather than
money, and carrying three positioning corrections from the author's site
research.

\subsection{The Two Sets Describe One Operation}

Set A and Set B are not two programmes. They describe the same hybrid procedure,
carrying surgical and medical oncology arms together.

What differs is what each leads with, and that is a claim about reviewers rather
than about science. A surgical reviewer asked to evaluate a drug schedule will
defer to the medical oncology literature, and the reverse holds too. Writing the
same trial twice, each time leading with what the reader can actually assess, is
the only honest way to ask both.

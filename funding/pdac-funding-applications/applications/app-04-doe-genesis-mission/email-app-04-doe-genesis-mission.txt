Dear DOE Office of Science, Genesis Mission Team,

I am submitting an unsolicited white paper as an independent scientist and
asking whether the Genesis Mission's Robotics line, or the American Science and
Security Platform's integration work, is the right place to route it.

The FY 2028 budget priorities memorandum annexed to "Science: A New Golden Age"
defines the Robotics national mission as "general-purpose autonomous systems
capable of dexterous manipulation, mobility, and reliable operation in real-world
environments, to initiate the era of physical AI-driven scientific discovery."
An eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy under a governed advisory model is
that sentence applied to a human operating room, which is the least forgiving
real-world environment available.

The proposal is a Phase 1, first-in-human, open-label, single-arm 3+3 escalation
in up to 18 participants with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS
G12-mutated pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, combining perioperative
daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) with the robotic operation and an on-premises language
model confined to advisory output. Three specific contributions are aimed at the
Mission rather than at the clinic.

First, a topology in which the model is useful and still cannot act. Everything
capable of influencing a motion sits inside the hospital trust boundary; records
leave for a public archive and nothing returns. The only path from advisory text
to a motion passes through a licensed human.

Second, a closed loop with one measured human gate. Both branches of the gate,
accepted and rejected, are recorded, so the rejection rate becomes a reported
endpoint rather than an internal metric.

Third, open formats. The advisory logging schema and the stop-latency
measurement method are released, not merely described, which is what the annex
asks federal purchasing power to drive across instrument vendors. Arm-level stop
is specified at 3 milliseconds and system-wide stop at 500 milliseconds.

Gold Standard Science is discharged artifact by artifact rather than asserted:
three verification record types, joined on case identifier, each carrying the
Executive Order 14303 principle it satisfies, released with the cohort including
negative results.

The request is $700,000 per year for five years, no cost share. Prior modeling
carries an ASME V&V 40 and ICH M15 aligned credibility score of 81.9 across 55
verification notebook tests. I intend to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer
Center at the feasibility stage only; UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or
endorser of this work.

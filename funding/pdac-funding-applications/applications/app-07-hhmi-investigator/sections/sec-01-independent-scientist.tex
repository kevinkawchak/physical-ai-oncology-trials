\section{Bet on People, Not Just Projects}

\textit{Science: A New Golden Age} asks the roughly \textbf{\$200 billion}
annual federal research portfolio to ``bet on people, not just projects''
\cite{kratsios2026sciencegoldenage}. Its Chapter II supplies the evidence for
why: HHMI-style person-based funding, roughly \$10 million over seven years,
produced high-impact publications at nearly double the rate of comparable
federally funded peers \cite{azoulay2011incentives}. The report's argument is
that the difference is not the money but the tolerance for a long horizon and
for early failure.

This inquiry asks whether that argument extends to an investigator with no
academic appointment. Between June 2025 and July 2026, one person produced
fourteen deposited works: the drug was identified from forty pancreatic cancer
meta-analyses, three quantitative trial simulations were run and audited, and a
complete clinical and regulatory package was written \cite{foundingdocs}. The
programme is not a set of aims. It is a single line of inquiry, held for a
decade, into whether the RAS pathway can be interrupted around a curative-intent
operation in a disease where five-year survival remains in the low teens
\cite{siegel2025statistics}.

Dear HHMI Investigator Program,

I am writing to ask a narrow eligibility question before anything else: whether
the Investigator Program can consider a scientist with no academic appointment,
and if not, what the Institute would regard as the minimum affiliation.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) asks the roughly $200 billion annual
federal research portfolio to "bet on people, not just projects," and it cites
HHMI-style person-based funding, roughly $10 million over seven years, as
producing high-impact publications at nearly double the rate of comparable
federally funded peers. The report's argument is that the difference is not the
money but the tolerance for a long horizon and for early failure. My inquiry is
whether that argument extends past the affiliation requirement.

The programme is one line of inquiry, not a set of aims: whether the RAS pathway
can be interrupted around a curative-intent pancreatic resection, in a disease
where five-year survival remains in the low teens. Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) has a
metastatic readout, RASolute 302 reporting median overall survival of 13.2
months against 6.6 for chemotherapy in the RAS G12 population. Nothing in that
readout speaks to the resectable setting, and the measurement that would settle
it can only be taken around an operation.

Written as states rather than aims, the programme has a property a project grant
cannot express: every failure transition re-enters the programme. If exposure is
inadequate the schedule changes and the agent is retained; if pathway
suppression is not demonstrated the agent changes and the question is retained.
Nothing leaves the diagram.

The record so far is one operator and fourteen deposited works between June 2025
and July 2026: two clinical protocols, an Investigational New Drug application,
two funding applications, five legislative drafts, and four guidance works. Three
quantitative trial simulations were run at an estimated $36,330 each against an
industry benchmark of $120,000 to $2,000,000 per run, and the digital-twin work
carries an ASME V&V 40 and ICH M15 aligned credibility score of 81.9 across 55
verification notebook tests. There is no institutional appointment and no
indirect cost behind any of it.

The first four years run through a Phase 1, open-label, single-arm 3+3
escalation in up to 18 participants, with clinical costs of roughly $700,000 per
year. I intend to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center at the
feasibility stage only; UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or endorser.

If the eligibility answer is no, I would be grateful for a sentence saying so
plainly, which is more useful to me than a general encouragement.

Sincerely,

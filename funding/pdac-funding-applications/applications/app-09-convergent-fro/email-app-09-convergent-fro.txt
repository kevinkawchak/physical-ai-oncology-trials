Dear Convergent Research,

I am writing to propose a focused research organization with a stated
dissolution date, and to ask whether the deliverable is the kind your FRO
programme exists to fund.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) describes focused research organizations
as time-bound nonprofit research startups built to dissolve, and places them in a
gap it defines precisely: mid-scale science, tens of millions of dollars,
coordinated teams of ten to a hundred, timelines of half a decade, which no
existing institution fills. The same chapter asks the roughly $200 billion
annual federal research portfolio to stop deferring to the incumbents that
consume it.

The deliverable is a dataset, not a company and not a laboratory: what the RAS
pathway does in human pancreatic tissue when a RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor
is given around a curative-intent resection. It is too operationally heavy for a
single academic laboratory, too unprofitable for a drug developer because it
differentiates no product, and finished once it exists.

Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) has a metastatic readout: RASolute 302 reported median
overall survival of 13.2 months against 6.6 for chemotherapy in the RAS G12
population. Nothing in that readout speaks to the resectable setting, and the
measurement that would settle it can only be taken around an operation.

Three properties I would ask you to test the concept against. First, the
organization closes in year five; the budget contains no year six, and year five
is a working year with its own deliverables rather than a wind-down. Second,
there are three releases and only one of them depends on the trial succeeding:
the verification harness lands in year two and is useful even if escalation
stops early, the specimen dataset lands in year four, and the full record
including negative and inconclusive cohorts lands in year five. Third, every
artifact has exactly one named recipient class agreed in year one rather than
negotiated in year five, which is the failure mode that strands a dissolved
organization's outputs.

The clinical vehicle is a Phase 1, open-label, single-arm 3+3 escalation in up
to 18 participants with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated
disease, with a staged eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy and an
on-premises language model confined to advisory output. The request is $700,000
per year for five years, $3,500,000 total, no cost share. I intend to partner at
UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center at the feasibility stage only; UC San Diego is
not a sponsor, site, or endorser of this work.

Sincerely,

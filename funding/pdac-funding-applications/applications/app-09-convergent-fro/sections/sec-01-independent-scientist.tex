\section{Built to Dissolve}

Chapter II of \textit{Science: A New Golden Age} describes focused research
organizations as time-bound nonprofit research startups built to dissolve, and
places them in a gap it defines precisely: mid-scale science, tens of millions
of dollars, coordinated teams of ten to a hundred, timelines of half a decade,
which no existing institution fills
\cite{kratsios2026sciencegoldenage}. The same chapter asks the roughly
\textbf{\$200 billion} annual federal portfolio to stop deferring to the
incumbents that consume it.

The output proposed here is a dataset, not a company and not a laboratory: what
the RAS pathway does in human pancreatic tissue when a RAS(ON) multi-selective
inhibitor is given around a curative-intent resection. It is too operationally
heavy for a single academic laboratory, too unprofitable for a drug developer to
fund on its own because it differentiates no product, and finished once it
exists. That combination is the definition of an FRO deliverable, and it is why
this organization states its dissolution date in its first sentence.

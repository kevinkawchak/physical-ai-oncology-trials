\section{The Individual Scientist and the \$200 Billion Realignment}

The July 2026 report to the President, \textit{Science: A New Golden Age},
states the position this application is written against
\cite{kratsios2026sciencegoldenage}. Its first overarching goal is that the
United States research system should prioritize the individual scientist over
legacy institutions: ``If we are serious about expanding what our scientists can
do, we must invest directly in American researchers and the bold ideas that
drive them. Too much of our research enterprise has come to serve itself rather
than the scientists within it.''

Chapter II attaches a number to that goal. Federal agencies distribute
approximately \textbf{\$200 billion} in annual research and development funding,
and the report finds that the portfolio has ``no systematic framework for
identifying where those dollars could catalyze the greatest scientific returns,
with deference instead given to the same incumbents that consume the funding.''
The corrective it names is to realign that portfolio toward risk taking, strip
away the administrative burden that consumes nearly half of an investigator's
working hours, and scale long-horizon individual grants ``modeled on National
Institutes of Health (NIH) Director's Pioneer Award.''

This application takes that literally. One person, working from one public
repository, has produced the package a program office produces with a team: two
protocols, an Investigational New Drug application, two funding applications,
five legislative drafts, and four guidance works \cite{foundingdocs}, none of it
carrying indirect cost at the 50 to 60 percent rates the report criticizes.

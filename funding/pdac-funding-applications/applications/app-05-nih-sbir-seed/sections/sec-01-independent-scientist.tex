\section{A Venture That Did Not Start With a Dissertation}

\textit{Science: A New Golden Age} asks the roughly \textbf{\$200 billion}
annual federal research portfolio to stop deferring to ``the same incumbents
that consume the funding'' and to open pathways ``grounded in demonstrated skill
rather than academic pedigree alone,'' naming SBIR as a program that ``opens
doors for technician-founded ventures, directing resources toward ideas that do
not always start with a dissertation'' \cite{kratsios2026sciencegoldenage}.

ChemicalQDevice is that kind of venture. Between June 2025 and July 2026 it
produced, with one operator, a Phase 1 protocol, a Phase 2 protocol, an
Investigational New Drug application, two funding applications, five legislative
drafts, and four guidance works, all publicly deposited \cite{foundingdocs}. The
commercial product is not the robot and not the drug. It is the governance
package that makes an on-premises model usable in a regulated operating room:
a logging schema, a bench stop-latency method, and a regulatory dossier a site
can adopt without rebuilding either.

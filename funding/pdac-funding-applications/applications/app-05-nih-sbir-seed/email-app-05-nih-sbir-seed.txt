Dear NIH SEED Team,

I am writing as the founder of a small business to ask which institute and which
SBIR omnibus topic best fit a governed on-premises surgical advisory system, and
whether a direct-to-Phase-II route is available given the work already completed.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) asks the roughly $200 billion annual
federal research portfolio to open pathways grounded in demonstrated skill
rather than academic pedigree alone, and names SBIR as the program that "opens
doors for technician-founded ventures, directing resources toward ideas that do
not always start with a dissertation." ChemicalQDevice is that kind of venture.

The commercial product is not the robot and not the drug. It is the governance
package that makes an on-premises language model usable in a regulated operating
room: an advisory logging schema, a bench stop-latency measurement method, and a
regulatory dossier that a qualified pancreatic surgery program can adopt without
rebuilding either the platform or the protocol. The clinical vehicle is a Phase 1,
first-in-human, open-label, single-arm 3+3 escalation in up to 18 participants
with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated pancreatic ductal
adenocarcinoma, combining perioperative daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) with a staged
eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy.

Phase I, nine months and $306,000, retires three technical risks: an interlock
that stops within 3 milliseconds per arm and 500 milliseconds system-wide, a
logging schema that captures every recommendation and replays a case from the
audit record, and a site workflow study with a dry run in a non-clinical theatre.
The gate into Phase II is a measured number, not a review. Phase II, 24 months
and $1,300,000, covers IND activation, monitoring, clinical conduct, escalation
to a recommended Phase 2 dose, and the public data release.

On unit economics, which is the part a Phase I reviewer actually underwrites:
industry benchmarks put a single virtual trial run above $120,000 and 4.5 months
and a ten-arm quantitative systems pharmacology trial above $2 million and
several months to a year. This company completed three such work products at an
estimated $36,330 each in about a month each.

I intend to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center at the feasibility stage
only; UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or endorser of this work. The attached
five-page inquiry carries the phased pathway, the risk dependency structure, the
cost benchmarks, and the budget by layer.

Sincerely,
CEO Kevin Kawchak
ChemicalQDevice

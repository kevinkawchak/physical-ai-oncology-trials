Dear NSF TIP Directorate,

I am writing to ask whether the X-Labs program is the right home for an
independent research organization I am forming, and what the current route is
for a concept outline from a performer with no degree-granting affiliation.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) describes X-Labs as the first federal
program designed to fund independent research organizations outside traditional
academia, and it asks the roughly $200 billion annual federal research portfolio
to stop deferring to the incumbents that consume it. The report's own
institutional-fit analysis is the argument I want to make.

The program is a Phase 1, first-in-human study combining perioperative
daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) with a staged eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy
and an on-premises large language model confined to advisory output. It needs
five activities held together: operating under an active IND, maintaining a
verification harness, publishing negative operative results, holding the
regulatory package as a maintained product, and releasing code and data openly.
A university department can do the first but not the fourth. A device company
can do the second and fourth but will not do the third or the fifth. A federal
laboratory can verify but does not operate. No existing performer type covers
all five, which is precisely the gap the X-Labs mechanism was created to fill.

The track record is a single operator. Between June 2025 and July 2026 I
identified the drug from forty pancreatic cancer meta-analyses, produced three
quantitative trial simulations, and wrote a complete regulatory package. The
August 2025 quantitative systems pharmacology run returned median overall
survival of 12.8 months against 5.4 for chemotherapy; RASolute 302 reported 13.2
against 6.6 months nine months later. I present that proximity as chronology and
not as validation, and I state the three differences wherever it appears.

The request is $700,000 per year for five years, no cost share, with a public
good released each year: the bench stop-latency method in year 1, the advisory
logging schema in year 2, the escalation dataset including negative results in
year 3, and the full safety and verification package in years 4 and 5. I intend
to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center at the feasibility stage only;
UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or endorser of this work.

The attached five-page concept outline carries the institutional-fit comparison,
the performer track record, the trial parameters, and the pathway including the
branch taken if the first site agreement does not execute.

Sincerely,

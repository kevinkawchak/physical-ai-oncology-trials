\section{A Performer That Does Not Exist Yet}

\textit{Science: A New Golden Age} makes an argument about organizations, not
only about money \cite{kratsios2026sciencegoldenage}. Federal agencies
distribute roughly \textbf{\$200 billion} in annual research and development
funding, and the report finds the portfolio defers to ``the same incumbents that
consume the funding.'' Its Chapter II answers with a table scoring universities,
corporate laboratories, federal laboratories, and new institutions against seven
activities, and it names the NSF TIP Directorate's X-Labs as ``the first federal
program designed to fund independent research organizations outside traditional
academia.''

A first-in-human physical AI surgical oncology program is a clean case for that
argument. A university department can run the trial but cannot maintain the
engineering, the verification harness, and the regulatory package as a product.
A device company can build the platform but will not publish negative operative
results. A federal laboratory can verify the software but does not operate on
patients. The work needs an organization that does all three and is obliged to
publish, which is the definition the report gives for a new institution.

Dear NCI Cancer Therapy Evaluation Program,

I am writing to ask how an unaffiliated investigator should route a Phase 1
concept in resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and whether CTEP would
review it in the absence of a cooperative group affiliation.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) uses this disease area as its own
example of scientific triumph, writing that "within living memory, cancer has
been transformed from a death sentence to a treatable condition for millions of
Americans." Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is where that sentence is least
true; five-year survival remains in the low teens. The report asks the roughly
$200 billion annual federal research portfolio to invest directly in individual
researchers, and its annex asks agencies to prioritise foundational biological
science over the broader life-sciences category.

The concept is a Phase 1, open-label, single-arm 3+3 dose
escalation of perioperative daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) in up to 18 participants
with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated disease, with a staged
eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy and an on-premises language model
confined to advisory output. Co-primary endpoints are dose-limiting toxicity
with determination of the MTD or recommended Phase 2 dose, and 30-day device- or
procedure-related serious adverse events.

The design point I would most like reviewed is attribution. A combined drug and
device study in a surgical population carries a structural hazard a standard
escalation does not: an adverse event may be attributable to the drug, to the
operation, or to the advisory system, and the dose decision depends on which.
Attribution is therefore adjudicated by a blinded reviewer before the toxicity
window closes. Only the drug branch reaches the dose decision; the surgical and
advisory branches feed the feasibility endpoint. Every branch reaches the
independent safety monitor without filtering.

Unusually for a concept, the protocol already exists and is published with a
DOI, as is the Investigational New Drug application, and the statistical and
safety plan is fixed rather than indicative. The correlative pharmacodynamic
work, plasma pharmacokinetics at days minus 14, minus 1 and plus 1, tumour
pathway pharmacodynamics in the resection specimen, ctDNA clearance, and
pathologic response grade, is costed inside the $700,000 per year budget rather
than deferred to a supplement. What is genuinely missing is a network and a
qualified site.

On the drug: RASolute 302 reported median overall survival of 13.2 months
against 6.6 for chemotherapy in the RAS G12 metastatic population. None of that
speaks to the resectable setting, which is what this concept proposes to measure
directly in the specimen. I intend to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer
Center at the feasibility stage only; UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or
endorser.

Sincerely,
CEO Kevin Kawchak

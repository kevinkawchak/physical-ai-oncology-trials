Dear Foundation for the NIH Partnerships Team,

I am writing as an independent scientist to propose a narrow, pre-competitive
consortium in resectable pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and to ask whether
FNIH would consider convening it.

"Science: A New Golden Age" (July 2026) holds up FNIH and the Accelerating
Medicines Partnership as its model for public-private structure, noting that the
Alzheimer's partnership experimentally validated twenty candidate drug targets.
Its annexed FY 2028 memorandum asks agencies to prioritise non-federal cost
share so that federal dollars draw in private investment rather than standing
alone. This concept is written to that shape.

The pre-competitive question is narrow and no party owns it: what happens
pharmacologically when a RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor is given around a
curative-intent pancreatic resection. Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) has a metastatic
readout, RASolute 302 reporting median overall survival of 13.2 months against
6.6 for chemotherapy in the RAS G12 population. None of that evidence speaks to
the resectable setting, and the measurement that would settle it, paired
pre-treatment and on-treatment human tumour tissue under controlled timing, can
only be obtained around an operation.

Three parties are required. I contribute a published Phase 1 protocol, an
Investigational New Drug application, an audited simulation package, and a fixed
analysis plan, all openly deposited. An academic cancer center contributes
patients, operative expertise, pharmacy, IRB, monitoring, and pathology. A drug
developer contributes investigational supply and a regulatory cross-reference.
The single shared output, a specimen-level perioperative pharmacodynamic dataset
covering plasma pharmacokinetics, tumour pathway pharmacodynamics, ctDNA
clearance kinetics, and pathologic response grade, is released openly and is
owned by none of the three. That is what makes it pre-competitive: it
differentiates no product.

The clinical vehicle is a Phase 1, open-label, single-arm 3+3 escalation in up
to 18 participants with resectable or borderline-resectable KRAS G12-mutated
disease, with a staged eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy and an
on-premises language model confined to advisory output. The cash ask is $700,000
per year for five years; drug supply, site infrastructure, pathology, and
bioanalytical support are contributed rather than purchased.

One scheduling dependency governs everything: the cross-reference letter must
precede the IRB submission, or the submission is repeated and the accrual window
is lost. I intend to partner at UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center at the
feasibility stage only; UC San Diego is not a sponsor, site, or endorser.

Sincerely,
CEO Kevin Kawchak
ChemicalQDevice

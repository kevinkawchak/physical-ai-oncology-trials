path. Figure 9 draws that path across the three parties it touches: the office

decision on the path is the first rule in action: if the company does not own
the answer, the letter goes to the office that does.

\subsection{What each letter asks}

The OSTP letter asks only which office is the right place for a small company to
offer service under direction \cite{ostp2026,ostpinfo2026}. The Genesis Mission
letter asks whether a small business can join a laboratory-led team as a
subcontributor, and whether clinical research is in scope
\cite{doegenesis2026,doegenesisfoa2026}. The Moores letter offers the advisory
software component to an investigator, under the university's procedures and
contracting route \cite{ucsdmoorescto2026,ucsdactri2026,ucsdocta2026}. The CTEP
follow-up reports one fact and asks for nothing \cite{ctep2026}. The OSTP letter
also copies the Office of Personnel Management's AI workforce team, because the
federal AI workforce programs are the other structure where a small AI company
could serve under direction \cite{opmaiworkforce2026,opmtechforce2026}.

\subsection{A note on the date}

September 30 is the last day of the federal fiscal year. If appropriations lapse
at midnight, some federal offices written to this week may not answer for a
time. Silence in that case is expected, and the three-business-day rule is
counted in days the recipient's office is open.

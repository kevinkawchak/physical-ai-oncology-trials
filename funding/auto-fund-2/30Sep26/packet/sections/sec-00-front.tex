\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

On September 12, 2026 the chief executive of ChemicalQDevice wrote to President
Trump about three priority changes for himself and the startup: obedience over
hard work and responsibilities; adaptation to existing authority, order, and
structure; and a teamwork-based early-rise, scheduled routine. Several
communications from the White House followed. This packet answers them.

It answers by turning each priority into an operating rule a counterparty can
check. When an office that owns a rule answers, the company follows the answer
before doing more of its own work. The company takes the role an existing
structure offers, even when that role is smaller than the one it asked for. And
its working day begins at 5:00 a.m. Pacific, so that its correspondence arrives
at the start of Washington's day. The White House is answered through its own
contact form \cite{whcontact2026}, and four letters then offer a contributor's
place inside four established structures: the federal science and technology
agenda coordinated by the Office of Science and Technology Policy
\cite{ostp2026}, the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission
\cite{eo14363genesismission}, the academic cancer center that would hold the
trial, and the National Cancer Institute's investigator-initiated pathway
\cite{ctep2026}.

None of the four letters asks to lead, to be funded, or to be endorsed. The
packet closes with the decisions inside the robotic Phase 1 and who holds each,
which is the same principle applied to the trial itself: every investigator
decision sits with the site, and the company keeps only the signature a sponsor
cannot delegate.

\subsection*{How to read this}

A reader with five minutes takes \S1, the message and the three rules, and \S2,
the action register with the path a letter follows under those rules.

A reader asking where the company stands takes \S3, the structure map, and \S4,
the early-rise weekday block by block.

A funder takes \S5, the decisions inside the Phase 1 and the evidence behind it.
\S6 carries the positioning constraints, the method, and the references, every
one of which is a clickable link.

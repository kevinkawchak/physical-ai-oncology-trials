\section{The Structure Map}

The second priority is adaptation to existing authority, order, and structure,
and a company cannot adapt to a structure it has not drawn. Table 13 names each
authority the program touches, what that authority governs, and the place the
company takes inside it. In every row the company's place is smaller than the
authority's, and that is the point of the table.

\subsection{Three rules the map implies}

\textbf{Every investigator decision sits with the site.} The company's chief
executive holds all of the sponsor, and so holds none of the decisions an
interested party could move. \S5 lists them.

\textbf{No authority is asked to do another's work.} The Securities and Exchange
Commission is not asked to judge clinical research, and the Cancer Therapy
Evaluation Program is not asked to fund a company concept. Each letter in this
block is addressed by that rule.

\textbf{The company's place is stated in writing before it is taken.} Each letter
to a structure says which place the company is offering to take, so that no
structure has to infer it. A row of Table 13 changes only when an authority
answers, and then it changes to the authority's own words.

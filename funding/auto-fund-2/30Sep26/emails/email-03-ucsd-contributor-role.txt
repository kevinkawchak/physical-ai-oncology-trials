Dear Moores Clinical Trials Office, ACTRI Clinical Trial Support Services, and Office of Clinical Trials Administration,

I wrote to Moores Cancer Center earlier about a proposed Phase 1 study of perioperative daraxonrasib with a staged robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy and an advisory-only on-premises language model. Since then I have learned something that changes the shape of what I should be asking, and I would rather tell you than let an earlier letter stand uncorrected.

The National Cancer Institute's Cancer Therapy Evaluation Program explained to me that its trials are investigator-initiated, and that a concept which originates with a company is the wrong shape by construction. I accept that without reservation. If a study of this kind is ever run at Moores, it belongs to a qualified Moores investigator, under the university's procedures, and my company's part is the advisory software component and the documentation around it. That is a contributor's role, and it is the one I am offering.

In practice that means four things.
1) The investigator directs the work, and the company follows the institution's standard operating procedures, schedules, and review decisions.
2) Any agreement follows whichever contracting route the Office of Clinical Trials Administration chooses, and the company signs nothing before that office has set the route.
3) The company's staff, if it has any, complete whatever training the university requires before they are engaged in research.
4) The company makes no public statement about Moores, UC San Diego, or any investigator without written permission.

My only question is who at Moores would be the right person to hear an offer of this kind, if anyone is. If the answer is that no investigator is interested, I would still be grateful to know, and I will not ask again.

Thank you for your time.

## Why a ledger now, years before a match is due

Two of the NSF tiers on the route to the pilot carry a matching requirement. A
Phase IIB supplement of $50,000 to $500,000 requires matching third-party
funding before NSF releases it, and the Strategic Breakthrough tier, up to $30
million, requires a 1 to 1 match. Both are years away. But a match is proven
with records, and records are easiest to keep from the first dollar rather than
to reconstruct from the last. This instruction opens the ledger and buys nothing.

## What counts, and what does not

| Source | Counts toward a third-party match? | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| An investor's cash investment in the company | Yes, if documented at the time | It is third-party funding into the business |
| A strategic partner's paid contract for the method | Yes, if documented | It is third-party revenue tied to the technology |
| The company's own reserve, including the Treasury ladder | No | It is the company's own money, not third-party funding |
| Federal award funds of any kind | No | Federal funds never match federal funds |
| In-kind promises with no signed document | No | A promise is not a record |

Confirm the definition against the solicitation in force on the day a match is
claimed; the table is the company's working rule, not a restatement of NSF's.

## The ledger, field by field

| Field | What is recorded |
|:--|:--|
| Date | The date the funds or the contract became binding |
| Counterparty | The legal name of the investor or partner |
| Instrument | Equity, a SAFE, a note, or a contract, with the document's title |
| Amount | The amount received or contracted, not the amount discussed |
| Document | Where the signed document is kept, never the document itself |
| Restrictions | Any condition on the funds that could disqualify them |
| Firewall note | Confirmation that no investor has any say in trial conduct, dose, or analysis |

The ledger is kept in the company's private records. This repository holds the
field list and never the entries.

## The one rule the ledger enforces

Private capital sits behind the firewall the capitalization plan sets: no
investor has any right over trial conduct, dose assignment, adjudication, or
analysis. A match that came with such a right would cost more than it was worth,
and the ledger's last column exists to catch it before it is accepted.

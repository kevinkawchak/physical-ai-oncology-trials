# The Eligibility Gate, and the Route Through It

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
For NSF program staff and the NCTI team. About 650 words.

---

## The inquiry

NSF has asked whether ChemicalQDevice would apply to a multi-million-dollar pilot
program. This brief sets out the pilots NSF has announced, the gate in front of
each, and where the company stands, so that the answer can be exact.

## The tiers and pilots

| Program | Amount | Gate | The company today |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| SBIR Phase I, NSF 26-510 | Up to $305,000 over 6 to 18 months | An invitation after a Project Pitch | Can pitch today |
| SBIR Phase II | Up to $1,250,000, typically 24 months | A completed Phase I | Not yet |
| Phase IIB supplement | $50,000 to $500,000, with third-party match | An active Phase II | Not yet |
| Commercialization Readiness Pilot, run by NCTI at UCF | $20 million over two years; six to eight companies at $1 million to $3.75 million | Active or recent NSF Phase II or IIB | Not yet |
| Strategic Breakthrough tier | Up to $30 million, with a 1 to 1 match | Phase II, after consulting the program officer | Not yet |
| Scientific instrumentation emphasis, NSF 26-511 | Part of a $40 million emphasis | Proposers in scientific instrumentation | Outside the company's technology |

## The route, step by step

| Step | What happens | Earliest realistic timing |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Project Pitch submitted | Day 4 of this block |
| 2 | NSF answers the Pitch by email | Weeks, not days |
| 3 | Full Phase I proposal, if invited | The next published deadline after the invitation |
| 4 | Phase I award and performance | 6 to 18 months |
| 5 | Phase II proposal and performance | Typically 24 months |
| 6 | Eligibility for the pilot or Strategic Breakthrough | During or after Phase II |

The route is long. It is also the only one the published rules describe, and a
company that asked for an exception to a pilot's gate in its first letter would
be asking NSF to set aside the structure that makes the pilot fair to the six or
eight companies it selects.

## What the company says, and what it does not

| It says | It does not say |
|:--|:--|
| It holds no NSF award | That it is eligible for the pilot |
| It reads the gate as requiring Phase II or IIB | That the gate should be waived |
| It is submitting a Pitch today | That the inquiry was a proposal invitation |
| It would join the institute's mailing list | That NSF has expressed interest in funding it |

## Why the honest answer serves the company

A program officer who wrote an inquiry is interested enough to have written. An
answer that shows the company has read the gate, knows where it stands, and has
already taken the first permitted step is the answer most likely to keep that
officer's interest through the eighteen months or more that the route takes. An
answer that claimed more would end it.

## What the pilot would buy, when the company reaches it

A milestone-based award of $1 million to $3.75 million with concierge-level
commercialization support is sized for exactly the gap this program has: the
$2,104,000 delta between the NIH SBIR route and the program's full five-year
cost. The company's capitalization plan places private capital against that
delta today; a pilot award reached after an NSF Phase II would reduce the private
share, not replace the federal one.

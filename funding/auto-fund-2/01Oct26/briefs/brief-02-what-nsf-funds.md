# What NSF Funds, and How the Program Is Split

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
For any reviewer asking how one program is divided between agencies. About 600
words.

---

## The rule that shapes the split

NSF's SBIR program funds technical innovation and does not fund clinical
trials. The National Institutes of Health fund clinical research, including
small-business work that leads to a trial. The program's private capital sits
behind a firewall and pays for what neither agency pays for. The split below
follows those three facts and nothing else.

## The split

| Component | NSF SBIR | NIH SBIR | Private capital |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Verification method for the advisory model | Yes, the Pitch | No | No |
| Boundary test suite and audit trail | Yes | Builds on the NSF result | No |
| Hardware-in-the-loop timing tests | Yes | No | No |
| Clinical protocol, IND, site activation | No | Yes | In part |
| Perioperative drug, surgery, and follow-up | No | Yes, through a site investigator | In part |
| Robotic platform access and site build-out | No | In part | Yes |
| Commercialization of the method | Later, through Phase II and the pilot | No | Yes |

## Why the scopes cannot overlap

The NSF work ends where a patient begins. It produces a verification method, a
test suite and measured failure rates on synthetic cases. The NIH work begins
where a patient begins: it uses the verified layer inside a trial that belongs to
a site investigator. If both were funded, the NIH work would cite the NSF results
rather than repeat them, and each application would disclose the other. Letter 4
of this day tells NIH so in writing, with NSF copied.

Neither is counted twice anywhere in the company's documents.

## What this means for the trial

The robotic Phase 1 is open-label, single-arm, 3+3 dose escalation of
perioperative daraxonrasib at 160, 220 and 300 mg in up to 18 participants, with
a staged eight-arm robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy. None of it is proposed to
NSF. What NSF would fund is the evidence that the advisory layer inside that
trial stays inside its boundaries, which is the evidence an institutional review
board and the FDA will ask for before the layer is allowed near a patient.

# The NSF Merit Review Map

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
For an NSF reviewer. About 600 words.

## The criteria

NSF reviews every proposal against two criteria, Intellectual Merit and Broader
Impacts, and SBIR proposals are also judged on their commercial impact. This
brief maps each to the evidence the company can put in front of a reviewer, and
names the evidence it does not yet have.

## Intellectual Merit

| What NSF asks | The company's evidence | What is missing |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Does the work advance knowledge? | A verification method that treats disagreement between independent models as a signal to withhold advice | Measured rates; that is the Phase I |
| Is the approach well reasoned? | A hard architectural boundary instead of instructions to the model; a credibility battery framed on ASME V and V 40 | An external reviewer's assessment |
| Is the team qualified? | A continuous, dated public record since June 2025, with persistent identifiers | Additional staff, planned on award |
| Are the resources adequate? | On-premises compute for the model; a request to the NAIRR Pilot for verification research | A reply to that request |

## Broader Impacts

| What NSF asks | The company's evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Benefit to society | A method that lets cancer centers use language models in regulated research only when the model is shown to stay in bounds |
| Public engagement and transparency | Every document, prompt and build record is public, and every figure in the company's papers is drawn from a public source |
| Economic competitiveness | A small American company building verification tools that trial sponsors and robotic surgery developers can license |
| Workforce | A staffing plan of eleven roles, six of which need no clinical license, and two unsolicited applicants who wrote before any posting |

## Commercial impact

| What NSF asks | The company's evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who is the customer? | Academic cancer centers, trial sponsors, and robotic surgery developers |
| Why would they pay? | Because a verifiable boundary is easier to place inside an existing regulatory clearance than an instructed one |
| What is the path to revenue? | Licensing the method and test suite, and services applying it to protocols |

## The quantitative anchors a reviewer can check

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Credibility score of the existing digital twin | 81.9 over 55 tests | The company's deposited simulation work |
| Simulated median overall survival, August 2025 | 12.8 against 5.4 months | https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17001137 |
| Reported median overall survival, Phase 3, May 2026 | 13.2 against 6.6 months, RAS G12 | https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2605555 |
| Force limits the layer must never touch | 3 N per arm, 18 N cumulative | The deposited Phase 1 protocol |
| Stop latencies the layer must never delay | 3 ms cross-arm, 500 ms system | Same |

The survival figures are a dated chronology, not a validation, and are presented
that way. They are included because they show the company's modeling is
serious enough to be worth verifying.

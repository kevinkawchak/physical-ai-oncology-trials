Three briefs for three questions a reader of the SEC request will ask: what
exactly is the record, by what measure could it be a standard, and what does the
sentence about OpenAI and Anthropic rest on.

## The three briefs

| # | File | Question it answers | Reader | Length |

| 1 | [`brief-01-the-request-and-the-record.md`](brief-01-the-request-and-the-record.md) | What is the record, and when was each part made? | The SEC office | About 750 words |
| 2 | [`brief-02-what-an-external-standard-requires.md`](brief-02-what-an-external-standard-requires.md) | By what eight criteria could it be a standard, and how does it score? | The FDA program, a technical reviewer | About 850 words |

## The scores brief 2 gives, stated once

| Criterion | Status |
|:--|:--|
| Persistent identifiers | Met |
| Dated versions | Met |
| Open method | Met |
| Credibility assessment | In part |
| Regulatory mapping | In part |
| Independent review | In part |
| Outcome validation | Not yet |
| Adoption, independently confirmed | Not yet |

A brief that scored every criterion as met would be read as advocacy. A brief
that says two are not yet met, and names the action that would change each, is
read as a record, which is what the request needs to be.

# The Weekly Cadence, Version 2

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
One page. For the chief executive and every future team member. It replaces
version 1 from the first block of funding actions.

---

## What changed, and why

Version 1 gave each weekday a theme and a time budget. The early-rise weekday
from day 3 of this block gave each day a timetable. This block also showed what
neither said: how to handle a week in which four signals arrive at once, and how
to decide which letters earn a second message. Version 2 joins the two and adds
the rules this week taught.

## The week

| Weekday | Theme of the two federal windows | The artifact that must exist by 4:00 p.m. |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Monday | Federal program offices and registrations | Sent letters; every registration confirmed current |
| Tuesday | Regulators, capital, and counsel | A capital decision recorded, or a written reason none was taken |
| Wednesday | Sites, partners, and the structures the company works inside | Sent letters; any meeting confirmed with an agenda |
| Thursday | Pilots, solicitations, and applications | Submissions made, and next week's drafts written and held |
| Friday | Earned follow-ups, and the week's record | Follow-ups sent; the record filed |

Every day keeps the same timetable: start at 5:00 a.m. Pacific, approval at
5:30, the first federal window from 6:00 to 8:00, technical work from 8:00 to
11:00, the West Coast window from 11:00 to noon, the second federal window from
12:30 to 2:00, build and commit from 2:00 to 3:30, and the record from 3:30 to
4:00.

## The follow-up rule

1. A letter earns one follow-up on the third business day after it was sent, counting only days the recipient's office was open.
2. A follow-up is sent only if no reply has arrived, and only if it carries one new fact.
3. A notice that asked for nothing earns no follow-up.
4. Two letters to the same office in one week share one follow-up.
5. A later letter to the same office supersedes an earlier one, and the clock starts again.
6. An applicant is never followed up. The company answers an applicant who writes, and waits for one who has not.
7. After one follow-up, the company writes again only when it is asked for something or has a new fact the office would want.

## The rules for a crowded week

**A signal is answered on the day its theme falls, not the day it arrives.** This
block answered an applicant signal on Monday, a regulator on Tuesday, the White
House on Wednesday and a pilot inquiry on Thursday, which is the frame of
version 1 exactly.

**No day answers two signals.** Two signals in one day would put two sets of
letters into one approval block, and the 5:30 approval is one decision, not two.

**Friday spends no new signal.** It sends only earned follow-ups and files the
record. A fifth day of new letters would bury the first four.

**A federal lapse moves the clock, not the letters.** If an office is closed,
its days do not count, and its follow-up waits until it has been open for three.

## What each week hands to the next

The record, filed on Friday, lists every letter, what it asked for, and the first
day it can next be acted on. The following Monday starts by reading that list at
5:00 a.m., before any new letter is drafted. That is the whole handover.

## What is deliberately not in the cadence

Chasing. Reading for its own sake. The technical work itself, which fills the
8:00 to 11:00 block and is not funding work. And any message to an office or a
person whose next move it is.

## The follow-up rule

A letter earns one follow-up on the third business day after it was sent,
counting only days the recipient's office was open, and only if no reply has
arrived. A notice that asked for nothing earns none. A follow-up carries one new
fact, or it is not sent. Where two letters went to the same office in one week,
one follow-up answers for both.

| Letters sent on | Third open business day | Follow-ups sent today |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Monday, September 28 | Thursday | 1, consolidated with Thursday's letter to the same office |
| Tuesday, September 29 | Today | 2: the SEC office and the FDA's Oncology AI Program |
| Wednesday, September 30 | Monday | None yet |
| Thursday, October 1 | Tuesday | None yet |

If appropriations lapsed on October 1, a federal office closed since then has
not had three open business days, and its follow-up waits. The two applicant
replies are not follow-ups: each is sent only if that applicant has answered.

## Positioning, carried into every file in this directory

A follow-up is a courtesy to an office that has not yet answered, not a
complaint, and no file here describes silence as a position. No word is
attributed to the SEC office beyond the two it wrote about the request, and the
follow-up to it repeats none. The two applicants are never named, and neither is
told that the other exists. Rasonque is approved in the metastatic setting, and
the perioperative use this program proposes remains investigational. Nothing in
[`investing/`](investing) is investment advice.

\section{The Follow-Up Rule}

\subsection{The rule}

A letter earns one follow-up on the third business day after it was sent,
counting only days the recipient's office was open. The follow-up is sent only
if no reply has arrived and only if it carries one new fact. A notice that asked
for nothing earns none. Two letters to the same office in one week share one
follow-up, and a later letter to an office supersedes an earlier one and starts
its clock again. An applicant is never followed up: the company answers an
applicant who writes, and waits for one who has not.

The rule is the first of the three priorities in the September 12 message,
applied to the company's own correspondence. Obedience over hard work means
waiting for the office that owns the answer rather than writing again because
writing is work that can be done.

\subsection{Why the NIH follow-up goes to NCI}

NIH received two letters this week, a personnel question on Monday and an overlap
disclosure on Thursday, both copied to the NCI SBIR Development Center. Two
follow-ups to one office would be one too many, so one consolidated letter
answers for both. It goes to the NCI center directly, because the application
would be assigned to the National Cancer Institute, and because the center asks
first-time Phase I applicants to send a Specific Aims page so that it can connect
them with a program director \cite{ncisbir2026,ncisbirpd2026}. The page is
summarized in \S4.

\subsection{What silence means}

Nothing. An office that has not answered in three days has taken no position,
and no file in this block describes silence as one. The follow-ups say so by
what they leave out: none repeats a question more sharply, none sets a deadline,
and each says it will not write again unless asked or unless it has a new fact.

# Naming Contingent Personnel in a Federal Application

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
For whoever drafts the next NIH or NSF small-business application. About 750
words.

---

## The situation

Two people have asked to work for the company. If either consents, the company
may want to name them in its next application as people who would join on
award. This brief sets out how that is done without overstating the team, and
what must be true first.

## What must be true before anyone is named

| Condition | Why |
|:--|:--|
| The person has consented in writing to be named | A name in a federal application is a representation about that person |
| The agency has confirmed how prospective personnel may be named | Asked in `../emails/email-01-nih-sbir-contingent-personnel.txt` |
| The person's role matches a role on the published staffing plan | A role invented for an applicant reads as padding |
| The person has seen the budget line and FTE for the role | So their letter of intent is to a real position |
| No other application names the same person for overlapping effort | Effort commitments across applications must not exceed what one person can give |

## The principal investigator rule

For an SBIR award, the principal investigator's primary employment must be with
the small business at the time of award and for the duration of the project. The
chief executive is the principal investigator and meets that rule himself. The
two prospective staff are not principal investigators, and the question put to
NIH is whether any comparable employment rule reaches them at the time of
application. Until NIH answers, the company assumes the conservative position:
they are described as prospective, never as current.

## How a contingent person is described

| Element | Wording pattern | Never |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Status | "Prospective [role], to be employed by ChemicalQDevice on award" | "Team member", "staff", "our coordinator" |
| Commitment | "Letter of intent, contingent on award, attached" | "Committed", "hired", "on board" |
| Effort | The FTE on the published plan for that role | An FTE chosen to make the budget look fuller |
| Qualifications | What the person's own materials show, in their words | Any qualification the company has not seen evidence of |

## The letter of intent

One page, signed by the person, on no letterhead of any other employer. It
states four things and nothing else.

1. The role, as named on the staffing plan, and its effort.
2. That the person intends to accept employment in that role if the award is made and an offer is extended.
3. That the intent creates no obligation on either party before then.
4. The date, and the person's signature.

The letter never names the person's current employer, because a current employer
is a third party that has agreed to nothing.

## How the two agencies differ

| Item | NIH SBIR Phase I | NSF SBIR Phase I |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Where the team is described | Research strategy and the senior or key personnel section, with biographical sketches | The Project Pitch's "Company and Team" field, then the full proposal |
| Space for the team at first contact | Full application | 1,750 characters in the Project Pitch |
| Ceiling | $306,000 total in the company's plan | $305,000 under NSF 26-510 |
| When the question is asked | Today, by letter 1 | Day 4, in the Project Pitch pack |

The NSF Project Pitch field is short enough that it can carry a sentence about
the two inquiries, and only a sentence: "Two prospective staff approached the
company without a posting, and have consented to be named on award." That
sentence is used only if both conditions in it are true on the day of
submission.

## What a reviewer will check

A reviewer who sees a prospective person in an application checks three things:
that the role exists in the budget, that the effort is plausible, and that the
company has not dressed intent up as capacity. Each of those is controlled by
the table above, and the third is controlled by one rule: **if a sentence would
still be true after both people declined, it can stay; if it would become false,
it must be qualified or removed.**

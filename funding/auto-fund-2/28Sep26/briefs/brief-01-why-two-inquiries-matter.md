# Why Two Unsolicited Inquiries Matter to a Funder

**ChemicalQDevice, San Diego.** Kevin Kawchak, CEO.
For a reviewer who scores the team. About 800 words.

---

## What happened

In the week of September 21, 2026 two people wrote to the chief executive on
LinkedIn asking to work for ChemicalQDevice. The company had posted no position
and published no hiring information: no job listing, no careers page, no
recruiter, and no post asking for applicants. Both people found the company
through its public work.

That public work is specific. Since June 2025 the company has deposited a
dated, public record around one molecule, daraxonrasib, while the molecule's
developer ran its own Phase 1/2 and Phase 3 trials. The FDA approved the
molecule as Rasonque on August 26, 2026. The company's papers were written
independently of the developer and at the same time as its trials, and each one
carries a date a reviewer can check.

## Why this is evidence, and of what

Federal small-business review asks whether the company can assemble the team its
budget pays for. A plan answers that question with intent. An unprompted inquiry
answers it with behavior, and behavior that happened before the company asked
for it is the hardest kind to manufacture.

| What a reviewer asks | What the inquiries show | What they do not show |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Does the company's work reach people outside it? | Two people read it and acted on it without being asked | How many others read it and did not write |
| Could the company recruit on award? | There is interest before any recruiting has started | That either person would accept an offer |
| Is the staffing plan realistic? | Two inquiries arrived for a plan of eleven roles | That either person is qualified for a specific role |
| Would the team work inside the program's structure? | The replies state the structured schedule up front | Anything, until each person answers |

The right column matters as much as the middle one. A company that presents two
inquiries as two hires has turned evidence into a claim, and a reviewer who
notices will discount everything else on the page.

## Why it matters more than its size

Two inquiries are a small number. They matter for three reasons that have
nothing to do with the number.

First, **they are unprompted.** The company did not post, advertise, or ask.
Evidence that arrives without solicitation carries no selection effect from the
company's own outreach.

Second, **they arrived before the award.** Most small businesses recruit after a
notice of award, and their reviewers must take the recruiting timeline on faith.
Here the pipeline exists before the money does, which shortens the time from
award to a working team.

Third, **they test the company's stated priorities.** On September 12, 2026 the
chief executive wrote to the President that the company now puts obedience
before hard work, has adapted to existing authority, order, and structure, and
keeps a teamwork-based early-rise schedule. The first two people who asked to
join are the first people to whom those priorities are stated as terms of work.
Both replies say so plainly.

## What the company does with them

| Step | Action | Condition |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Send both the same reply, in the LinkedIn conversation each opened | None |
| 2 | Ask each for a resume, the closest role, and consent to be named | None |
| 3 | Ask NIH how a Phase I application may name prospective personnel | None |
| 4 | Name a consenting person in an application, contingent on award | Consent, and NIH's answer |
| 5 | Make an offer, with the pay range stated | An award, and a structured interview |

Nothing in steps 1 to 4 creates an obligation on either side. Step 5 happens only
after an award.

## How the robotic Phase 1 uses these roles

The trial a funder is buying is open-label, single-arm, 3+3 dose escalation with
up to 18 treated participants, daraxonrasib at 160, 220 and 300 mg, and a 28-day
dose-limiting toxicity window, with a staged eight-arm robotic
pancreaticoduodenectomy and an on-premises language model confined to advisory
output. Six of its eleven roles require no California clinical license, and
those six are the roles an unsolicited applicant can realistically fill:

| Role | FTE on award | Why the trial needs it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Director of clinical operations | 0.40 | Closes the activation checklist and owns the procedure index |
| Lead clinical research coordinator | 1.00 | Runs consent, visits and source documents every working day |
| Regulatory affairs and quality manager | 0.45 | Corresponds with the review board and the agency, and holds the archive |
| Robotics and physical AI systems engineer, site safety officer | 0.55 | Owns the force envelopes, the interlocks and the emergency stop chain |
| LLM verification and model governance lead | 0.40 | Holds the advisory-only boundary and the audit trail |
| Data manager and biostatistician | 0.30 | Convenes cohort review and locks the database |

Those six sum to 3.10 of the 3.95 award-funded full-time equivalents. The other
five roles are the chief executive and four licensed clinicians who would come
from the host institution.

## What is not claimed

Neither person has been hired, interviewed, or evaluated. Neither is named in
any document the company has published. No position is open. The inquiries are
reported as what they are: two unprompted expressions of interest, received
before the company asked anyone.

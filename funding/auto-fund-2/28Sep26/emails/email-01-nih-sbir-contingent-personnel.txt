Dear NIH SBIR and STTR Program Staff,

I am writing with one narrow question about personnel in a Phase I application. I have copied the NCI SBIR Development Center and NIH SEED because the application I have in view would most likely be assigned to the National Cancer Institute.

ChemicalQDevice is a one-person San Diego company preparing a Phase I application for a governed, advisory-only software system that supports a staged robotic pancreaticoduodenectomy with perioperative daraxonrasib. I wrote to NIH SEED and the SBIR program after the FDA approved daraxonrasib as Rasonque for metastatic pancreatic cancer, and nothing in that letter has changed. The perioperative use I propose remains investigational.

The new fact is small but concrete. Recently two people wrote to me, each on their own initiative, asking to work for the company. I had posted no position and published no hiring information. The staffing plan behind my applications names eleven roles at 3.95 full-time equivalents, most of them part-time and all of them funded only on award, and both people appear to fit roles in that plan.

My question is how, if at all, a Phase I application may name such people.

1) May an application name a person who is not yet an employee of the small business as senior or key personnel, or as another significant contributor, supported by a letter of intent that is contingent on the award?
2) I understand the principal investigator must be primarily employed by the small business at the time of award and for the duration of the project, and I will be the principal investigator. Please confirm that no comparable employment requirement applies at the time of application to other named personnel who would be hired only after an award is made.
3) If such a person is named, is a biographical sketch required in the same form as for a current employee, and is there wording NIH prefers for describing a position that is contingent on funding?

I am not asking for an assessment of the program, and I am not asking you to review either person. I would rather name nobody than name people in a way that misrepresents the company's team to a review panel.

Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,
CEO Kevin Kawchak

Three letters, nine verified addresses, and one question per recipient. None of
them asks anyone to fund anything today. Each asks the office that owns a rule
how that rule applies to a company that has two unsolicited applicants and no
open position.

## Why letter 2 has three recipients with three different questions

A first employer's questions do not belong to one agency. The SBA owns small
business counseling, USCIS owns the federal employment verification system, and
California owns its own job-creation credit. Three separate letters would draw
three answers that do not know about each other; one letter with three labeled
questions lets each recipient answer only its own and see what the others were
asked. The letter says so in its first paragraph, so no recipient reads the
copy as a mistake.

## How to send one

1. Open the `.txt` file on GitHub, select everything between `=== BODY ===` and `=== END BODY ===`, and copy it.
2. Paste into a new message in iOS Mail. Each paragraph is one line in the file, so the phone wraps it to the screen with no stray breaks.
3. Copy the `TO`, `CC` and `SUBJECT` lines into their fields separately.
4. Attach the files named under `ATTACHMENTS COMPILED FROM THIS DIRECTORY`.
5. Work through `BEFORE SENDING` line by line.

| That Rasonque is approved for perioperative use | It is approved in the metastatic setting only |

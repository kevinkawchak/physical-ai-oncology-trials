One instruction, and it buys nothing today. It renames the purpose of the
shortest rung of the company's existing Treasury ladder so that, if an award is
made and an offer accepted, the first paychecks are already covered for the 90
days both NIH and NSF allow before an award's start date.

## What changes today

| Item | Before | After |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Rung A, about 3 months | A liquidity rung with no named purpose | The contingent payroll earmark |
| Purchases | None | None, unless Rung A's par is below $40,000 |
| Rungs B to D, the sleeve, the residual | Unchanged | Unchanged |

## The one number

**$40,000** is the largest 13-week earmark any two of the six roles an applicant
could fill would need: the lead clinical research coordinator at $22,000 and the
systems engineer at $18,000. It comes from the published staffing budget divided
by four, and it is not re-derived anywhere else.

| 1 | [`capital-01-contingent-payroll-earmark.md`](capital-01-contingent-payroll-earmark.md) | Designate Rung A; top it up at the next 13-week bill auction only if its par is below $40,000 | No |

## Positioning

Nothing here is investment advice or a recommendation to any other person. The
reserve is the company's own capital. Federal award funds are never deposited
into the brokerage account, and the earmark is spent only after an award letter
and an accepted offer, inside the agencies' 90-day pre-award window.

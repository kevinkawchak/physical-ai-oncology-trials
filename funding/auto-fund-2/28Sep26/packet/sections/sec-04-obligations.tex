\section{Obligations Before and After a First Hire}

A company that has never employed anyone has no policy history, so its first two
conversations become that history. This section lists what California and
federal law require of an employer from the moment an applicant writes, and it
sets the rule this company follows where the law does not yet reach a business
of its size. Table 4 is ordered by when each obligation begins.

The first three rows apply today, to two people who have written but have not
been offered anything \cite{calab4323,cagov12952}. Both LinkedIn replies are
drafted against them: neither asks about pay, both offer the pay range on
request, and neither asks anything about the applicant beyond their experience
and their consent to be named. The Fair Chance Act binds employers of five or
more, and the company has one member and no employees. It follows the rule
anyway, because a policy adopted before it is required cannot later be said to
have been adopted for a particular person.

\subsection{Equal treatment, by construction}

The two replies are identical in substance, and the interview plan asks both
applicants the same six questions in the same order, scored 0, 1 or 2 against
the same written rubric for a total out of 12. Two of the questions test the
first two of the chief executive's stated priorities: a time the candidate
followed a procedure they disagreed with, and who decides when a procedure in a
regulated study may be changed. A third asks what about an early-start,
scheduled weekday would be hard, and the only wrong answer to it is a false one.

A total is a record, not a decision. No offer follows any score until an award
is made, and the scoring notes are written during the conversation rather than
reconstructed afterward.

\subsection{Privacy}

Neither applicant is named in this packet, in the repository that holds it, or
in any letter sent today. The files refer to Applicant A and Applicant B in the
order their messages arrived. The first name each reply needs is typed into the
LinkedIn message box at the moment of sending and never written into a committed
file. Resumes, notes and scores are kept in the company's private applicant log
for the four years California requires.

\subsection{Employee, not contractor}

Both roles, if offered, are employee positions paid through payroll. California
decides whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor with the ABC
test in Labor Code 2775, and a coordinator or engineer who works the company's
schedule, under its procedures, in its usual course of business fails every prong
of the contractor side. Neither applicant is offered a contractor arrangement as
a way to start sooner, however much sooner it would be.

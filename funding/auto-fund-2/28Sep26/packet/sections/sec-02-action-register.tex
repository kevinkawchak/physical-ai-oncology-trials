\subsection{What the three letters ask, and of whom}

Each letter goes to three offices, and each office is asked only what it owns.
The National Institutes of Health are asked whether a Phase I application may
name a person who is not yet an employee, supported by a letter of intent that
is contingent on award, and in what form \cite{nihsbirseed,nihgpssmallbiz}. The
Small Business Administration, the E-Verify Contact Center and California
Competes are each asked one labeled question in a single letter, so that each
answer is given knowing what the others were asked
\cite{sbacontact2026,everify2026,calcompetes2026}. The Moores Clinical Trials
Office, ACTRI and the university's Office of IRB Administration are asked what
training a research staff member who is not a university employee needs before
being engaged in research \cite{ucsdmoorescto2026,ucsdactri2026,ucsdirb2026}.

None of the three letters names or describes either applicant. The applicants
wrote privately and have agreed to nothing, including appearing in a letter to a
federal office. The letters ask about rules, and the rules are the same whoever
the people turn out to be.

\subsection{What is deliberately not done today}

No position is posted. A posting written because two people asked would be a
posting written for two people, and it would invite applicants to whom a
California employer must give a pay scale on request, for a role the company
cannot yet fund \cite{calab4323}. No interview is scheduled, because an
interview implies a decision that an award has not yet made possible. No form is
filed, because both employer packs create obligations that begin only with a
first payroll \cite{eddregister2026,eddnewhire2026}.

\actionbox{The one approval this day asks for}{Approve the same reply to both
applicants, in the LinkedIn conversations they opened, and the three letters
that open routes to fund them. No offer is made, no interview is promised, no
position is posted, and neither person is named anywhere outside the
conversation they began.}

\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

In the week of September 21, 2026 two people wrote to the chief executive of
ChemicalQDevice on LinkedIn, each on their own initiative, asking to work for the
company. The company had posted no position and published no hiring information.
Both found it through its public work: a dated record of papers, protocols and
applications built around daraxonrasib, the molecule the FDA approved on August
26, 2026 as Rasonque \cite{fdarasonque2026}.

This packet spends that signal. It answers both applicants with the same reply,
places them against the eleven-role staffing plan that the company's federal
applications would fund \cite{movein2026}, lists the employer obligations that
begin the day a first offer is accepted, and opens four routes that could pay
for a first hire. Three letters go out today, each to three offices that own a
rule the company needs answered. No offer is made, no interview is promised,
and neither applicant is named anywhere in this document or in the public
repository that holds it.

The packet's argument is narrow and checkable. A reviewer who scores a small
business asks whether it can assemble the team its budget pays for. Two
inquiries that arrived before the company recruited anyone are evidence on that
question, and this packet states exactly what they show and what they do not.

\subsection*{How to read this}

A reader with five minutes takes \S1, the signal and why it is spent first, and
\S2, the action register that ends in the one approval this day asks for.

A reader who scores teams adds \S3, the eleven roles and the six an unlicensed
applicant can fill, and \S5, the four routes that could fund a first hire
together with the robotic Phase 1 those roles would run.

A reader checking compliance takes \S4, the obligations before and after a
first hire. \S6 carries the positioning constraints, the method note, and the
references, every one of which is a clickable link.

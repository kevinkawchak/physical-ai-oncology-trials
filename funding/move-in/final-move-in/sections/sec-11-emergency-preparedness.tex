\section{Scope and Command Structure}

This plan covers fire, seismic events, power and utility loss, cybersecurity
incidents, model faults, and robot faults, and the continuity of operations
after any of them. It uses the eleven roles named in document 14 §1 and creates
no new titles.

\section{Fire and Evacuation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item On alarm, all robot instances receive a hardware stop under document 09 §3
  and are left in place. No attempt is made to power down a robot in an orderly
  sequence during an alarm.

\item Egress follows the routes established in the life safety plan approved at
  commissioning gate C5 \cite{nfpa101}. There are two means of egress from each
  procedure suite.

\item A participant under anesthesia is moved by defend-in-place to the adjacent
  smoke compartment, and is evacuated from the building only on the order of the
  incident commander after consultation with clinical command.

\item Assembly is at the designated point outside the loading area. The
  ambulance approach described in document 10 §4 is kept clear at all times and
  is not used for assembly.

\item Accountability is by badge reader roll call for the restricted and machine
  zones, and by sign-in sheet for the clinical zone.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Seismic and Structural Events}

\begin{enumerate}
\item During shaking, personnel take cover and no procedure is started. A
  procedure already in progress is stopped at the earliest safe point on the
  order of clinical command.

\item After shaking stops, the site safety officer performs a documented walk of
  every restricted zone before any robot instance is re-energized.

\item Robotic operations may not resume until every instance in the affected
  suite has passed an interlock test and an envelope survey, and the robot
  instance register has been amended under document 06 §4. A seismic event is a
  register-amending event even where no fault is found, because the absence of a
  fault is itself the finding that has to be recorded.

\item Structural inspection by a licensed engineer is required before
  re-occupancy where the recorded ground motion exceeds the threshold in the
  facility's post-event inspection procedure.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cybersecurity Incident and Model Fault}

These are two distinct events with two distinct responses, and treating them as
one is the error this section exists to prevent.

\begin{mvtable}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.4cm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.7cm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\headrow \thead{Event} & \thead{First action} & \thead{What continues}\\
\midrule

\end{mvtable}
\tabcapl{tab:cyber}{Five incident classes. The second and third rows carry the
whole design: a model fault does not stop the trial, it stops the advisory
output, because the human pathway was never dependent on the model.}

Response follows the identify, protect, detect, respond, and recover functions
of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 \cite{nistcsf2}. The fault taxonomy for
model output is the verification-before-generation taxonomy established in the
2026 legislative work \cite{vvuqbill}. A model fault is reported to the
department under document 06 §6 and appears in the department's annual report to
the Legislature under document 01 §6.

\section{Business Continuity, Drills, and Training}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The recovery point objective for trial data is 15 minutes and the recovery
  time objective is 4 hours, achieved by continuous replication to a
  geographically separate store.

\item The site maintains a written mutual aid arrangement with at least one
  clinical facility within 30 minutes travel time for participant transfer, and
  with one specimen storage facility. The intended partner of choice sits 1.6
  miles away, but no agreement exists, so the arrangement is with whichever
  facility executes one.

\item Drills are conducted quarterly on a fixed rotation: quarter one, robot
  fault during an active procedure; quarter two, cybersecurity incident and
  model fault; quarter three, building evacuation with a participant under
  anesthesia; quarter four, extended power failure and continuity activation.

\item Every drill produces an after-action record reviewed by the incident
  commander, and any corrective action enters the quality system under document
  12 §6.

\item Emergency preparedness training is 8 hours annually for all personnel, and
  is part of the training hours fixed in document 12 §5. This document does not
  set its own hours.
\end{enumerate}

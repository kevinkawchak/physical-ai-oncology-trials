## Overview

This paper organizes proposed solutions to problems that may arise as large language models (LLMs), robotics, and other physical-AI systems are explored for oncology clinical-trial workflows. It connects prior publications and prototype work across trial protocols, regulatory documentation, funding applications, patient advocacy, verification and validation, simulation, interoperability, and research synthesis.

The repository and linked publications are research materials. They should not be interpreted as evidence that an AI system or robot is clinically validated, approved for autonomous patient care, or ready for deployment. Clinical, regulatory, engineering, and ethical review remain necessary.

## At a glance

- **Clinical-trial planning:** proposed Phase I/II protocol drafts and investigational documentation.
- **Site readiness:** move-in and regulatory-documentation packages.
- **Funding workflows:** NIH/NSF/DOE-related application materials and supporting documents.
- **Patient and public interests:** advocacy materials, proposed policy frameworks, and patient-journey documentation.
- **Trustworthiness:** verification, validation, and uncertainty quantification (VVUQ).
- **Technical infrastructure:** Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, federated learning, and cross-system interoperability.
- **Computational research:** digital twins, quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP), in-silico trials, and literature synthesis.
- **Operational concepts:** proposed readiness levels and task-substitution analyses.

## How to use this repository

1. **Start with the paper and its source files.** Review the publication and its bibliography to understand the motivation and scope of each proposed solution.
2. **Open the linked publication records.** Confirm the version, date, methods, limitations, and supporting files for each item before citing or reusing it.
3. **Separate proposals from evidence.** Identify whether an item is a conceptual framework, generated document, simulation, software prototype, preprint, or validated clinical result.
4. **Review the underlying implementation.** For software or simulation claims, inspect code, dependencies, input data, tests, reproducibility notes, and validation results where available.
5. **Use formal review for real-world applications.** Clinical protocols, device use, trial conduct, regulatory submissions, and patient-facing materials require qualified human review and the appropriate institutional and regulatory approvals.

## Suggested review workflow

For any proposed LLM or robotics application in an oncology trial, reviewers can document:

- **Intended use:** the exact task and the decisions the system is allowed to support.
- **Inputs and provenance:** source documents, data permissions, versioning, and data quality.
- **Model and system configuration:** model/version, prompts, tools, code, robot hardware, and software dependencies.
- **Verification:** tests showing that the implementation behaves as specified.
- **Validation:** evidence that the system is fit for its intended context of use.
- **Uncertainty and failure modes:** known limitations, edge cases, fallback behavior, and escalation to qualified staff.
- **Human oversight:** accountability, approval points, audit logs, and stop conditions.
- **Security and privacy:** access controls, data minimization, retention, and applicable privacy requirements.
- **Change control:** review and revalidation after model, prompt, software, data, or hardware updates.
- **Reproducibility:** versioned artifacts, documented methods, and repeatable evaluation.

This checklist is a general review aid, not a substitute for applicable clinical, regulatory, cybersecurity, or research-governance standards.

## Safety, validation, and interpretation

- **Not medical advice:** this repository is for research and informational use; it does not provide patient-specific medical advice.
- **Not authorization to treat:** inclusion here does not establish that a drug, protocol, robot, model, or workflow is approved, safe, effective, or clinically deployable.
- **Human accountability:** qualified clinical, engineering, statistical, regulatory, and ethics professionals must independently assess relevant work.
- **Simulation limits:** simulated results depend on assumptions, data, code, and model behavior; they do not establish real-world efficacy or safety.
- **LLM limitations:** generated text and code can be incorrect, incomplete, inconsistent, or non-reproducible. Verify material claims against authoritative sources and independently test generated code.
- **Patient privacy:** do not upload identifiable patient data or confidential trial information to tools unless use is specifically authorized and meets applicable privacy and security requirements.
- **Regulatory status:** check current official guidance and approvals before using any material in a submission or clinical workflow.
